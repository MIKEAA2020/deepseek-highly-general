The deepest upgrade is not another geometric layer. It is to replace the claimed **curvature–survival equivalence**, which is generally false, with a precise theory of **viability-weighted holonomy in an endogenously self-maintaining system**.

This preserves the strongest insight—noncommuting adaptation can consume viability margin—while closing the major mathematical and empirical gaps.

# 1. Necessary corrections before extending the theory

## 1.1 Curvature is not equivalent to survival

The proposed theorem

\[
\text{survival under perturbation}
\iff
\text{bounded curvature}
\]

cannot hold in general.

- A flat connection can transport an agent directly out of its viable set.
- A highly curved connection can remain entirely inside a large viable region.
- Curvature controls the discrepancy between transports around alternative or closed paths; it does not control the absolute trajectory along each path.
- Endpoint holonomy does not guarantee that the agent remained viable during the loop.
- A norm bound on curvature is usually sufficient only after combining it with loop area, viability margin, transport regularity, and disturbance bounds.

The rigorous replacement should be:

> **Curvature predicts adaptation hysteresis; viability margins determine whether that hysteresis is fatal.**

That is both stronger scientifically and more defensible mathematically.

---

## 1.2 Fisher-minimal inequality projection is not automatically a connection

Suppose the agent chooses a policy velocity \(v\) by minimizing Fisher cost subject to viability inequalities. The map

\[
\dot b\longmapsto v
\]

will generally be piecewise nonlinear because constraints enter and leave the active set. An Ehresmann connection, by contrast, requires a smoothly varying linear horizontal subspace.

Therefore the framework has two regimes:

1. **Constant active-set strata:** a genuine smooth connection can be defined.
2. **Constraint-switching boundaries:** the dynamics form a stratified or hybrid projected system, not one globally smooth connection.

Calling the whole object a smooth information-geometric connection would introduce a mathematical error. The correct object is a:

> **stratified viability transport law**, carrying a smooth connection on each constant-rank active stratum and explicit transition rules between strata.

---

## 1.3 A fully observable POMDP is an MDP

If the grid state is fully observable, use “MDP.” If observations are partial or noisy, use “POMDP.”

More importantly, a finite grid is not naturally a differentiable base manifold. Differential curvature should therefore not be defined directly on grid squares in physical state space. Instead, define a continuous experimental-control manifold such as

\[
\Theta\subset \mathbb R^2,
\]

whose coordinates might be:

- food scarcity,
- hazard intensity,
- sensor noise,
- energy price,
- repair latency.

Plaquettes are then loops in \(\Theta\), while the agent acts inside the grid-world at each parameter value.

---

## 1.4 Hard-coded survival constraints do not establish autopoiesis

An agent constrained by externally imposed conditions such as

\[
E>0,\qquad I=1
\]

is homeostatic, but not yet demonstrably autopoietic. Autopoiesis requires the system to maintain the mechanisms that maintain those constraints.

The distinction is:

- **Homeostasis:** the agent remains inside a prescribed viable set.
- **Autopoiesis:** the agent regenerates the sensing, control, repair, and boundary-maintenance machinery required to remain there.

This gap should be closed experimentally rather than rhetorically.

---

# 2. The upgraded mathematical object

Call the revised object a **Stratified Autopoietic Viability-Geometric System**, or SAVGS.

A SAVGS consists of

\[
\mathcal S=
(\Theta,\mathcal P,g_F,\mathcal K,\mathcal T,\mathcal R,\Gamma).
\]

## 2.1 Experimental base space

\[
\Theta\subset\mathbb R^d
\]

is a manifold of controlled environmental conditions. A point \(\theta\in\Theta\) specifies an environment family, not an instantaneous grid location.

This makes infinitesimal loops, area scaling, and curvature well-defined.

---

## 2.2 Policy fibers

For each relevant state or observation \(s\), let

\[
p_s\in \operatorname{int}\Delta^{A-1}
\]

be the agent’s stochastic policy. The total policy space is a product of open simplices,

\[
\mathcal P
=
\prod_{s\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\operatorname{int}\Delta^{A-1}.
\]

If all fibers are identical, the bundle \(\Theta\times\mathcal P\to\Theta\) is topologically trivial. This is not a defect: nontrivial holonomy can arise from a non-flat connection on a trivial bundle. The theory should not imply nontrivial bundle topology unless varying action supports or transition singularities actually produce it.

---

## 2.3 Fisher geometry without logit gauge errors

On one simplex, the Fisher metric is

\[
g_F(u,v)
=
\sum_a \frac{u_a v_a}{p_a},
\qquad
\sum_a u_a=\sum_a v_a=0.
\]

For a state-weighted policy space,

\[
g_F(U,V)
=
\sum_s w_s
\sum_a
\frac{U_{sa}V_{sa}}{p_{sa}},
\]

where the weights \(w_s>0\) must be fixed in advance or defined by an independently specified reference distribution. Using the agent’s changing occupancy distribution without acknowledging it would make the metric itself path-dependent.

The square-root embedding

\[
\psi_a=2\sqrt{p_a}
\]

maps the simplex into the positive orthant of a sphere of radius \(2\). The exact Fisher–Rao distance is therefore

\[
d_F(p,q)
=
2\arccos\!\left(\sum_a\sqrt{p_aq_a}\right).
\]

This avoids softmax gauge ambiguity. If logits are nevertheless used, impose a gauge such as

\[
\sum_a \ell_a=0,
\]

because \(\ell\) and \(\ell+c\mathbf 1\) represent the same policy and the full logit Fisher matrix is singular.

---

## 2.4 Viability and maintenance states

Let the complete internal state be

\[
x=(p,z,r),
\]

where:

- \(p\) is the policy,
- \(z\) contains regulated quantities such as energy and integrity,
- \(r\) contains repair and maintenance machinery.

Define the viable set

\[
\mathcal K
=
\{(\theta,x):h_\alpha(\theta,x)\ge 0,\ 
\alpha=1,\ldots,m\}.
\]

Use nonzero margins, for example

\[
E\ge E_{\min}>0,
\qquad
I\ge I_{\min},
\qquad
R_j\ge R_{j,\min},
\]

rather than merely \(E>0\). Strict inequalities do not define a closed safe set and are unsuitable for standard viability theorems.

# 3. A rigorous Fisher-minimal transport law

On a constant active-set stratum, suppose the active viability constraints are represented locally by

\[
c(\theta,p)=0.
\]

Let

\[
J_p=D_pc,\qquad J_\theta=D_\theta c.
\]

For an environmental velocity \(\dot\theta\), define the policy velocity as the Fisher-minimal solution of

\[
J_pv+J_\theta\dot\theta=0
\]

that minimizes

\[
\frac12 v^\top G(p)v.
\]

If \(J_p\) has full row rank, the unique solution is

\[
v
=
-
G^{-1}J_p^\top
\left(
J_pG^{-1}J_p^\top
\right)^{-1}
J_\theta\dot\theta.
\]

Thus

\[
\dot p=-A_i(\theta,p)\dot\theta^i,
\]

with

\[
A_i
=
G^{-1}J_p^\top
\left(
J_pG^{-1}J_p^\top
\right)^{-1}
\partial_i c.
\]

This is a genuine horizontal transport law on a smooth, constant-rank stratum.

When inequality constraints switch activity, the theory must transition to a projected differential inclusion such as

\[
\dot p\in
\arg\min_{v\in T_{\mathcal K}(\theta,p;\dot\theta)}
\frac12\|v-v_0\|_{F,p}^2,
\]

where \(T_{\mathcal K}\) is the appropriate contingent cone. This part is viability dynamics, not ordinary smooth connection theory.

---

# 4. The central novelty: viability-weighted curvature

Raw curvature magnitude is not the biologically relevant quantity. Curvature may point along a harmless policy direction. What matters is the component of holonomy that erodes a specific viability margin.

For horizontal vector fields

\[
H_i=\partial_{\theta^i}-A_i,
\]

define the vertical curvature

\[
F_{ij}=[H_i,H_j]^{\mathrm{vert}}.
\]

In coordinates this contains both derivative and noncommutative terms,

\[
F_{ij}
=
\partial_iA_j-\partial_jA_i+[A_i,A_j],
\]

with the precise signs determined by the chosen horizontal-lift convention.

For a viability function \(h_\alpha\), define its local curvature susceptibility by

\[
\kappa_\alpha(\theta,x;u,v)
=
\frac{
\left[-D_ph_\alpha\!\left(F(u,v)\right)\right]_+
}{
h_\alpha(\theta,x)
},
\]

whenever \(h_\alpha>0\).

The aggregate index is

\[
\kappa_V(\theta,x)
=
\sup_{\substack{u,v\\ \|u\wedge v\|=1}}
\max_\alpha
\kappa_\alpha(\theta,x;u,v).
\]

This quantity has a direct interpretation:

> \(\kappa_V\) is the worst fractional loss of viability margin per unit oriented environmental-loop area, to leading order.

It is more meaningful than \(\|F\|\) alone because it contracts the geometric defect with experimentally specified survival covectors \(D h_\alpha\). The contraction is coordinate-independent when all objects are transformed consistently.

This is the most substantial conceptual advance available to the framework.

# 5. A corrected theorem

## Small-loop viability–holonomy theorem

Let:

1. \(\Theta\) be a smooth two-dimensional experimental manifold.
2. \(\mathcal P\) be an interior policy simplex with Fisher metric.
3. The active constraint rank be constant in a neighborhood of \((\theta_0,p_0)\).
4. The Fisher-minimal horizontal lift be \(C^2\).
5. \(h_\alpha(\theta,p,z,r)\) be \(C^2\).
6. A square loop \(\gamma_\varepsilon\) of side length \(\varepsilon\) remain in that neighborhood.
7. The non-policy states either return to their initial values or their residual changes are included explicitly in the expansion.

Then parallel transport around the loop satisfies

\[
p_{\mathrm{end}}-p_0
=
\varepsilon^2F_{12}(\theta_0,p_0)
+
O(\varepsilon^3),
\]

up to the orientation/sign convention for \(F\). Consequently,

\[
h_\alpha(x_{\mathrm{end}})
-
h_\alpha(x_0)
=
\varepsilon^2
D_ph_\alpha
\!\left(F_{12}\right)
+
O(\varepsilon^3)
+
\Delta_\alpha^{\mathrm{other}},
\]

where \(\Delta_\alpha^{\mathrm{other}}\) contains any non-returning energy, integrity, repair, or environmental components.

If

\[
h_\alpha(x_0)
+
\varepsilon^2D_ph_\alpha(F_{12})
-
C_\alpha\varepsilon^3
-
|\Delta_\alpha^{\mathrm{other}}|
>0
\]

for every \(\alpha\), then the endpoint remains viable.

### What this theorem does not claim

It does not guarantee viability at every intermediate point. For pathwise survival one additionally needs either:

- a positive viability tube around the entire horizontally lifted path, or
- a Nagumo-type inward-pointing condition on \(\partial\mathcal K\), with robustness slack exceeding implementation disturbances.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{transport tangency controls immediate survival;}
\quad
\text{curvature controls path-dependent viability erosion.}
}
\]

Curvature is neither necessary nor sufficient for survival by itself.

# 6. Repeated-loop fatigue: a genuinely new prediction

The theory becomes more distinctive when loops are repeated.

Let loop \(k\) have oriented area \(a_k\) and local viability-weighted curvature \(\kappa_{V,k}\). To leading order, repeated noncommuting adaptation consumes a fraction of available margin bounded by approximately

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k\kappa_{V,k},
\]

plus higher-order, drift, and stochastic terms.

A conservative sufficient condition is

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\left(
a_k\kappa_{V,k}
+
C_k a_k^{3/2}
+
\eta_k
\right)
<1,
\]

where \(\eta_k\) bounds estimation and implementation error in units of fractional viability margin.

This yields a testable phenomenon:

> An agent may survive every environmental transition in isolation yet fail after repeated closed cycles because noncommuting adaptations accumulate irreversible organizational drift.

That is stronger and more novel than merely saying “large curvature causes death.” It predicts **geometric adaptation fatigue**.

If reversed loops cancel the drift to leading order, the mechanism is geometric. If they do not, secular learning, resource depletion, or irreversible damage is dominating.

# 7. Closing the autopoiesis gap

## 7.1 Endogenous maintenance graph

Introduce a bipartite graph

\[
\Gamma=(\mathcal M,\mathcal R,\mathcal E),
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal M\) contains maintained components: sensors, policy updater, boundary, energy converter, repair modules;
- \(\mathcal R\) contains processes that produce, repair, or enable those components;
- edges encode consumption, production, catalysis, and enablement.

A minimal operational closure criterion is:

1. Every indispensable component \(m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ess}}\) degrades at a positive rate.
2. Every such component is regenerated by at least one internal process.
3. Every internal regeneration process is enabled by components belonging to the same organizational closure.
4. External inputs may supply matter and free energy, but not preassembled replacements for indispensable internal organization.

Graphically, the indispensable maintenance subgraph should lie in a productive strongly connected component. Graph closure alone is not sufficient, however; the associated fluxes must also support a feasible positive steady state.

---

## 7.2 Intervention-based closure test

For each purportedly self-maintained component \(m_j\):

1. Set its internal repair flux to zero.
2. Keep external energy and raw-material supply unchanged.
3. Observe whether the system loses the capacity to maintain other essential components.
4. Restore the repair pathway and test recovery.

A component is causally internal to organizational closure only if its intervention has the predicted downstream effect.

This prevents “autopoiesis” from being satisfied by a simulator that silently repairs the agent from outside.

---

## 7.3 Closure-aware viability curvature

The viability functions should include maintenance machinery:

\[
h_j^{\mathrm{repair}}=r_j-r_{j,\min}.
\]

Then curvature can be projected specifically onto maintenance vulnerabilities:

\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{closure}}
=
\sup_{\|u\wedge v\|=1}
\max_{j\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ess}}}
\frac{
[-D h_j^{\mathrm{repair}}(F(u,v))]_+
}{
h_j^{\mathrm{repair}}
}.
\]

This distinguishes:

- harmless behavioral hysteresis,
- metabolic viability erosion,
- degradation of the machinery that makes future adaptation possible.

Only the third is specifically autopoietic failure.

# 8. A falsification protocol that avoids circularity

## 8.1 Experimental parameter plane

Use two independently controllable environmental parameters,

\[
\theta=(\theta^1,\theta^2),
\]

for example:

- \(\theta^1\): food scarcity,
- \(\theta^2\): hazard intensity.

At each \(\theta\), the agent operates in the same grid-world architecture.

Run oriented loops

\[
\theta_0
\to
\theta_0+\varepsilon e_1
\to
\theta_0+\varepsilon(e_1+e_2)
\to
\theta_0+\varepsilon e_2
\to
\theta_0.
\]

Also run the reverse orientation.

---

## 8.2 Independent estimation and prediction

Do not estimate curvature from the same closed-loop endpoint used to “validate” it.

Split the experiment:

### Calibration data

Estimate local transport operators from independent open-edge perturbations:

\[
\theta\rightarrow\theta+\varepsilon e_i.
\]

Fit \(A_i\), constraint derivatives, and uncertainty intervals.

### Held-out test data

Compose the independently estimated edge transports to predict the policy after an unseen loop:

\[
\widehat U_\gamma
=
\widehat U_4\widehat U_3\widehat U_2\widehat U_1.
\]

Then compare this prediction with actual loop execution.

This prevents tautological agreement.

---

## 8.3 Empirical holonomy

Let \(p_0\) be the initial policy and \(p_\gamma\) the post-loop policy. Define the empirical holonomy in the Fisher tangent space by

\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{emp}}
=
\log^{F}_{p_0}(p_\gamma).
\]

Subtract a matched no-loop drift control:

\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{corr}}
=
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{emp}}
-
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{hold}}.
\]

The geometric prediction is

\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{geo}}
=
\varepsilon^2\widehat F_{12}
+
O(\varepsilon^3).
\]

A natural discrepancy statistic is

\[
T
=
\frac{
\left\|
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{corr}}
-
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{geo}}
\right\|_F
}{
\widehat{\sigma}_{\mathrm{total}}
},
\]

with uncertainty obtained from independent repeated trials or a hierarchical bootstrap over stochastic trajectories.

---

# 9. Required controls and failure signatures

A credible experiment should test all of the following.

## 9.1 Orientation reversal

For sufficiently small loops,

\[
\mathcal H(\gamma^{-1})
=
-\mathcal H(\gamma)+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]

Failure of sign reversal indicates ordinary drift, asymmetric damage, or model misspecification rather than local curvature.

## 9.2 Area scaling

For square side \(\varepsilon\),

\[
\|\mathcal H\|_F=O(\varepsilon^2).
\]

Test several loop sizes. A leading \(O(\varepsilon)\) effect indicates failure to close the loop, calibration bias, or unremoved secular learning.

## 9.3 Commuting control

Choose two perturbations expected to induce commuting updates. The predicted leading holonomy should be approximately zero.

## 9.4 No-learning control

Freeze policy updates while retaining energy expenditure and environment transitions. This measures physical depletion around the loop independently of adaptation holonomy.

## 9.5 Equal-exposure permutation test

Compare protocols with identical exposure durations and environmental marginals but different order:

\[
1\to2
\quad\text{versus}\quad
2\to1.
\]

A difference isolates noncommutativity from total exposure.

## 9.6 Subdivision consistency

A large loop prediction should agree, within finite-loop error, with the ordered composition of several smaller loops. This tests whether the local connection meaningfully predicts finite transport.

## 9.7 Viability projection

Do not test only whether the policy holonomy matches. Test whether

\[
D h_\alpha\bigl(
\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{corr}}
\bigr)
\]

predicts the observed change in each held-out viability margin. Otherwise the geometry may be correct but biologically irrelevant.

---

# 10. Explicit falsification hierarchy

The theory should be split into independently falsifiable claims.

### Claim A: Local transport law

Open-edge policy changes agree with Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving transport.

**Falsified if:** another preregistered transport model consistently predicts held-out edge updates better.

### Claim B: Holonomy law

Held-out loop drift agrees with ordered geometric transport and shows orientation reversal and area scaling.

**Falsified if:** corrected loop drift remains \(O(\varepsilon)\), fails orientation reversal, or lies outside predictive uncertainty.

### Claim C: Viability relevance

The contraction of holonomy with viability gradients predicts margin erosion better than raw Fisher displacement or exposure-only baselines.

**Falsified if:** \(\kappa_V\) provides no out-of-sample predictive gain.

### Claim D: Repeated-loop fatigue

Repeated loops accumulate viability loss according to ordered local holonomy bounds.

**Falsified if:** failure probability is explained entirely by cumulative resource expenditure, irrespective of loop orientation and order.

### Claim E: Autopoietic closure

Internal maintenance interventions produce the causal dependencies predicted by \(\Gamma\).

**Falsified if:** indispensable modules are effectively restored by hidden external mechanisms, or if knockout effects do not follow the claimed closure graph.

This modular structure ensures that failure of the grand interpretation does not obscure which mathematical component failed.

# 11. What would count as genuinely profound novelty?

The strongest defensible novelty is the combination of four elements:

1. **Stratified Fisher-minimal viability transport**, rather than an informally named connection.
2. **Viability-weighted curvature**, rather than raw geometric curvature.
3. **Repeated-loop geometric adaptation fatigue**, rather than a false curvature–survival equivalence.
4. **Interventionally verified endogenous maintenance closure**, rather than hard-coded homeostasis relabeled as autopoiesis.

Together, these support a precise thesis:

> Adaptive systems are endangered not merely by large environmental changes, but by noncommuting sequences of individually manageable changes whose induced policy holonomy is aligned with vulnerable self-maintenance directions.

That claim is mathematically meaningful, algorithmically implementable, and experimentally falsifiable.

# Revised final verdict

The project should no longer claim that survival is equivalent to bounded information-geometric curvature. Its rigorous result is narrower and more powerful:

> On smooth constant-active-set strata, Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving adaptation defines a connection whose curvature predicts the leading-order policy hysteresis generated by closed environmental perturbations. Survival depends on whether that holonomy, together with along-path disturbances and resource costs, exhausts the system’s viability and self-maintenance margins.

The decisive empirical test is therefore not merely whether empirical and theoretical holonomy agree. It is whether independently estimated, gauge-invariant, viability-weighted holonomy predicts held-out margin erosion, orientation reversal, area scaling, and repeated-loop failure after ordinary energetic depletion has been controlled.

If those predictions fail, the geometric mechanism fails. If they hold—and if the agent demonstrably regenerates its own maintenance machinery—the framework would advance from metaphorical “algorithmic life” to a genuine experimental theory of path-dependent self-maintenance.
