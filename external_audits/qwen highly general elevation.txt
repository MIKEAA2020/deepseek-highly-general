# 1. Verification of the audit points

## Overall verdict

The audit is **correct on essentially every major mathematical objection**. The manuscript contains a real and publishable core:

> On a smooth constant-active-set stratum, Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving adaptation can define a transport law whose curvature predicts leading-order policy holonomy/hysteresis; survival consequences require a separate viability-margin analysis.

The manuscript currently overreaches by identifying several distinct objects:

- an average viability deficit with curvature;
- Chentsov’s theorem with a \(CO(n-1)\) structure group;
- a policy simplex with a principal bundle;
- a composite optic with an endofunctor;
- numerical convergence with Banach contraction;
- a filtered colimit with an inverse limit;
- a node-reappearance test with autopoiesis;
- Kolmogorov/algorithmic rate-distortion with a smooth differentiable observable;
- a scalar divergence invariance with Noether’s theorem;
- trace-distance scaling with Zeno survival-probability scaling.

None of these problems requires abandoning the broad program. They require **typing the objects, constructing the missing bundles/functors/metrics, separating curvature from viability depth, and replacing tautological validations with independent predictions**.

## Point-by-point validation

### 1. The defined \(\kappa_V\) is not curvature

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Definition 2.1 defines  
  \[
  \kappa_V(\gamma_a)
  =
  \frac{1}{1}\int_0^1 (V_{\max}-V\circ\gamma_a(t))\,dt,
  \]
  which is an average viability deficit.
- Proposition 4.4 later defines \(\kappa_V\) as a positive directional derivative of a Bregman divergence evaluated at an algorithmic rate-distortion distance.
- No equivalence is proved.

Mathematical status:

- The Definition 2.1 object has no orientation, no connection, no commutator, no 2-form structure, and is parametrization-dependent unless arclength or constant-speed parametrization is fixed.
- A flat connection can have nonzero average viability deficit.
- Therefore Definition 2.1 is not curvature.

**Required repair:** introduce two separate objects:

\[
D_V(\gamma)
=
\frac{1}{|\gamma|}
\int_\gamma
(V_{\max}-V)\,ds,
\]

a loop-depth or viability-deficit functional; and

\[
\kappa_V(u,v)
=
\max_\alpha
\frac{[-\,d m_\alpha(F(u,v))]_+}{m_\alpha},
\]

a viability-weighted curvature functional built from a genuine curvature 2-form \(F\) and viability-margin functions \(m_\alpha\).

The radial example may satisfy \(D_V(a)=a^2\) and \(H_{\mathrm{geo}}(a)=\pi a^2\), but that is a model-specific relation, not a definition of curvature.

### 2. The Fisher stabilizer is incorrectly identified as \(CO(n-1)\)

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Definition 2.2 states that for the Fisher–Rao metric on the \(n\)-dimensional simplex, the stabilizer is  
  \[
  G_C=CO(n-1)=\mathbb R_+\ltimes SO(n-1).
  \]
- It attributes this to Chentsov’s theorem.

Mathematical status:

- Chentsov’s theorem gives uniqueness of the Fisher metric under statistical-map invariance.
- It does not imply positive-scale invariance.
- The Fisher metric is a Riemannian metric; its local orthonormal frame bundle has structure group \(O(n-1)\), or \(SO(n-1)\) after orientation.
- A reduction to \(CO(n-1)\) requires an explicit conformal/scale structure.

**Required repair:** state:

\[
G_{\mathrm{frame}}\subseteq O(r),
\]

for an \(r\)-dimensional policy fiber; if oriented,

\[
G_{\mathrm{frame}}\subseteq SO(r).
\]

If one adds an endogenous scale variable, e.g. effective precision, temperature, sample size, or Weyl factor, then one may define a conformal frame bundle with structure group

\[
CO(r)=\mathbb R_+\ltimes SO(r).
\]

But \(CO(r)\) must be a declared modeling structure, not a consequence of Chentsov alone.

### 3. The proposed principal bundle is not constructed

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Definition 3.1 gives a “policy fiber”  
  \[
  \pi:\Delta^{n-1}\to\Theta.
  \]
- This direction is wrong for a bundle over \(\Theta\). A bundle requires  
  \[
  \pi:E\to\Theta
  \]
  with fibers \(\pi^{-1}(\theta)\).

Mathematical status:

- A policy simplex is not automatically a principal \(CO(n-1)\)-space.
- A principal \(G\)-bundle requires a free transitive \(G\)-action on each fiber.
- The manuscript does not define total space, transition functions, or group action.

**Required repair:** use one of the following explicit constructions.

The simplest is a trivial policy bundle:

\[
E=\Theta\times \mathcal P,
\qquad
\pi(\theta,p)=\theta,
\]

where \(\mathcal P\) is the policy manifold, e.g. the open simplex \(\Delta^{m-1}_\circ\), \(S^1\), \(SO(3)\), or a reduced policy space.

If a principal structure is desired, define either:

1. the orthonormal frame bundle of the vertical Fisher metric:
   \[
   \operatorname{Fr}(E/\Theta)\to E,
   \]
   with structure group \(O(r)\) or \(SO(r)\);

2. a conformal frame bundle with structure group \(CO(r)\), after introducing a scale bundle;

3. or a trivial principal bundle
   \[
   P=\Theta\times G\to\Theta
   \]
   with explicit connection one-form.

The last is often enough for prototype holonomy calculations.

### 4. “Stratified connection” is only locally established

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- It says the formal 2-categorical gluing, coherence, and holonomy well-definedness are left for future work.
- Nevertheless the abstract and main proposition speak of a stratified connection as though globally established.

Mathematical status:

- Fisher-minimal constrained updates are smooth only while the active constraint set and rank are constant.
- At active-set switching, the dynamics become hybrid, projected, or a differential inclusion.
- A 2-categorical span is not a proof of global holonomy unless the gluing maps, coherence cells, and boundary conditions are constructed.

State the main theorem only on constant-active-set strata. For global statements, either:

1. construct a projected dynamical system or differential inclusion at switching boundaries;

3. define holonomy through switching loops as a path-ordered composition of stratum holonomies and boundary reset maps;
4. or explicitly exclude switching loops from the main theorem.

The unfinished 2-categorical object should be presented as a proposed extension, not as an established framework.

### 5. Algorithmic rate-distortion is not a usable differentiable quantity

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Definition 4.1 defines
  \[
  \operatorname{dist}_D(x)
  =
  \min\{|p|:U(p)=\hat x,\ d(x,\hat x)\le D\}.
  \]
- Proposition 4.4 differentiates a Bregman divergence evaluated at this quantity.

Mathematical status:

- \(\operatorname{dist}_D(x)\) is a shortest-program length.
- It is machine-dependent up to additive constants.
- It is generally uncomputable.
- It is integer-valued or discrete.
- It is not a smooth state variable.
- It cannot be inserted into a directional derivative without regularization or smoothing.

The claim that it “eliminates the deterministic-versus-random coding gap” is only partly meaningful: it changes the object from ensemble rate-distortion to single-string description length, but it does not produce a differentiable operational curvature.

**Required repair:** choose one of the following.

#### Option A: finite-code description length

Use a finite, computable code family and define

\[
L_D^N(x)
=
\min\{\ell(c): c\in C_N,\ \operatorname{dec}(c)=\hat x,\ d(x,\hat x)\le D\}.
\]

This is still discrete but computable.

#### Option B: smooth surrogate

Define a softened rate-distortion potential, for example

\[
r_{\tau,\beta,D}(x)
=
-\tau
\log
\sum_{c}
2^{-\ell(c)/\tau}
\exp\left(
-\beta [d(x,\operatorname{dec}(c))-D]_+^2/\tau
\right).
\]

For \(\tau>0\), under smooth decoder/distortion assumptions, this is \(C^2\) or smoother. Then define

\[
h_\alpha(\theta,x)
=
D_\phi(r_{\tau,\beta,D}(x),r_{\tau,\beta,D}(x_0)).
\]

Now Proposition 4.4-style curvature formulas are meaningful.

#### Option C: keep Kolmogorov complexity theoretically

Retain \(\operatorname{dist}_D\) only as an upper-semicomputable theoretical bound, not as an empirical single-trajectory differentiable observable.

**Elevated path:** use Option B for operational theorems and Option C for asymptotic upper bounds. The algorithmic version becomes a conjectural upper envelope unless a smooth envelope theorem is proved.

### 6. The Bregman-Noether proposition is false as stated

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Proposition 5.1 claims that because a Bregman divergence is invariant under a one-parameter affine transformation, Noether’s theorem yields a conserved current.
- The Lagrangian
  \[
  L=D_\phi(d,\tilde d)+\lambda D_\phi(p,\tilde p)
  \]
  has no configuration space, velocities, boundary conditions, or action integral.

Mathematical status:

- Noether’s theorem applies to differentiable actions on a configuration space with a Lagrangian depending on trajectories and velocities.
- Invariance of a scalar divergence under coordinate transformation does not by itself create a conserved current.
- Bregman divergences are not invariant under arbitrary affine transformations while holding the generator fixed; the generator must transform appropriately.

**Required repair:** replace Proposition 5.1 with a genuine symmetry-momentum theorem.

Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^r\) be open convex, \(\phi\in C^3\) strictly convex, and

\[
g_\phi(q)=\nabla^2\phi(q)
\]

the Hessian metric. Consider the geodesic Lagrangian

\[
L(q,\dot q)
=
\frac12 g_\phi(q)(\dot q,\dot q)-U(q).
\]

Let \(\xi\) be a vector field generating affine transformations preserving the Hessian metric:

\[
\mathcal L_\xi g_\phi=0,
\]

and preserving the potential:

\[
\xi(U)=0.
\]

Then Noether’s theorem gives the conserved momentum

\[
J_\xi(q,\dot q)
=
g_\phi(q)(\dot q,\xi(q)).
\]

If the affine flow preserves \(\phi\) only up to an affine function,

\[
\phi\circ g_t=\phi+\ell_t,
\]

then the Bregman divergence is invariant in the appropriate paired sense, and the same symmetry argument applies to the Hessian metric.

This supplies a valid replacement for the “Bregman-Noether correspondence”: not divergence invariance alone, but affine isometries of the Hessian metric yield conserved momenta.

### 7. The optic-category theorem is a category error

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Construction 7.1 lists seven optics \(O_1,\dots,O_7\).
- Theorem 7.4 claims that their composition is an endofunctor
  \[
  T:\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C)\to\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C).
  \]
- The proof only proves associativity of optic composition.

Mathematical status:

- A composite optic is an optic, not automatically an endofunctor.
- To define an endofunctor one needs an object map, morphism map, identity preservation, and composition preservation.
- The compatibility condition \(A_i=M_{i+1}\) is not sufficient to define a functor on the entire optic category.
- The table mixes sets, CPTP maps, metric tensors, residuals, and scalars without a common typing discipline.

**Required repair:** separate three notions.

#### Correct minimal theorem: composite endo-optic

Let \(\mathcal I\) be a typed interface category with objects

\[
I_0,I_1,\dots,I_7,
\]

and an interface isomorphism

\[
\sigma:I_7\cong I_0.
\]

Let

\[
O_i:I_{i-1}\rightleftarrows I_i
\]

be typed optics. Then

\[
O
=
\sigma\circ O_7\circ\cdots\circ O_1
\]

is a well-defined endo-optic on \(I_0\).

This is true by associativity and unitality of optic composition.

#### Stronger valid theorem: endofunctor on a category of periodic systems

Define a category \(\mathsf{Sys}_7\) of seven-periodic typed systems. An object is a tuple

\[
(X_1,\dots,X_7,f_1,\dots,f_7),
\]

where \(f_i:X_i\to X_{i+1}\), with \(X_8=X_1\). A morphism is a compatible family of maps.

If each arc \(O_i\) is lifted to a functor

\[
F_i:\mathsf{Sys}_i\to\mathsf{Sys}_{i+1},
\]

then the composite

\[
\mathcal T=F_7\circ\cdots\circ F_1
\]

is an endofunctor on a suitably identified periodic category. Its fixed point, if it exists, is a coalgebra

\[
X\cong \mathcal T(X).
\]

This preserves the ambition but requires explicit functorial semantics.

#### Operational fixed point theorem

Alternatively, let \(R\) be a realization functor from endo-optics to endomaps on a complete metric space \(S\). If

\[
T=R(O):S\to S
\]

is a contraction, then \(T\) has a unique fixed point by Banach’s theorem.

This is the clean way to keep the fixed-point claim without claiming that an optic is automatically an endofunctor.

### 8. The Banach argument does not establish a fixed point

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Section 13 reports numerical convergence of
  \[
  T_{\mathrm{reg}}(K)
  =
  (1-\lambda)T(K)+\lambda\Pi_K(T(K)).
  \]
- It concludes that “the unification object exists by Banach’s theorem.”
- No global Lipschitz bound is proved.

Mathematical status:

- Observed geometric convergence is not a contraction proof.
- \(R^2\) of a tail fit is not a Lipschitz constant.
- \(\Pi_K\) depending on the current set \(K\) makes the operator definition suspect.
- A fixed point of the regularized operator need not be a fixed point of the original operator.
- Finite grids do not prove contraction on continuous spaces.

**Required repair:** prove an explicit bound

\[
d(Tx,Ty)\le q d(x,y),
\qquad q<1,
\]

on a complete metric space.

A clean sufficient theorem is:

Let \(X=[0,1]^d\) with Euclidean metric. Let

\[
f=f_7\circ\cdots\circ f_1:X\to X
\]

with each \(f_i\) Lipschitz and

\[
L=\prod_{i=1}^7 \operatorname{Lip}(f_i)<1.
\]

Let \(C\subset X\) be closed and convex, and let \(\Pi_C\) be the Euclidean projection. Define

\[
T_\lambda(x)
=
(1-\lambda)f(x)+\lambda \Pi_C(f(x)),
\qquad
0\le\lambda<1.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{Lip}(T_\lambda)\le L<1,
\]

because \(\Pi_C\) is nonexpansive. Therefore \(T_\lambda\) has a unique fixed point.

If the manuscript cannot prove \(L<1\), it should say:

> Numerical evidence suggests contraction of the regularized update, but Banach existence has not been proved.

To elevate the claim, provide analytic Jacobian bounds or Lipschitz constants for each optic realization.

### 9. The prototype changes the meaning of \(n\)

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- At \(n=3\), the policy simplex is said to be two-dimensional and the structure group \(SO(2)\).
- Later the \(n=3\) agent has base coordinates \((x,y)\) and a scalar policy heading \(\theta\in S^1\).
- At \(n=4\), the state space becomes \(\mathbb R^3\) but the policy heading remains \(\theta\in S^1\), while the structure group is declared \(SO(3)\).

Mathematical status:

- A scalar heading has structure group \(SO(2)\), not \(SO(3)\).
- To get an \(SO(3)\) non-Abelian signature, the policy fiber must carry a three-dimensional rotational frame, e.g. \(SO(3)\), \(S^2\) with frame bundle, or a three-dimensional policy simplex after square-root embedding.

**Required repair:** introduce separate notation.

Use:

- \(m\): number of policy actions; policy simplex dimension \(m-1\);
- \(d\): environmental/base dimension;
- \(r\): policy fiber dimension;
- \(n\): total ambient state dimension, if needed, \(n=d+r\);
- \(G\): structure group determined by the actual policy fiber geometry.

For the non-Abelian prototype, choose one of:

1. base \(B=\mathbb R^3\), policy fiber \(SO(3)\), structure group \(SO(3)\);
2. base \(B=\mathbb R^3\), policy fiber \(S^2\), frame bundle \(SO(3)\);
3. policy simplex \(\Delta^3_\circ\), square-root sphere \(S^3_+\), local frame group \(SO(3)\).

Do not use a scalar \(S^1\) heading to claim \(SO(3)\).

### 10. Holonomy, area, curvature, and Gauss–Bonnet are conflated

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Remark 9.1 states that \(H_{\mathrm{geo}}(a)=\pi a^2\) is the loop area and calls the equality a “Gauss–Bonnet collapse.”
- But no connection one-form is explicitly constructed.
- Gauss–Bonnet concerns integrated Gaussian curvature and topology, not an arbitrary policy connection.

Mathematical status:

- The area law \(H=\pi a^2\) follows only if one specifies a connection whose curvature is the standard area form.
- A generic \(S^1\)-bundle connection need not have this property.
- Noncommutativity of \(SO(3)\) is not the same as base-space curvature; matrix commutators and curvature 2-forms must be related explicitly.

**Required repair:** construct the connection.

For the abelian prototype, take

\[
B=\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
P=S^1,
\qquad
E=B\times S^1.
\]

Let \(\psi\) be the policy heading. Define the connection one-form

\[
\alpha=d\psi + A,
\]

with

\[
A=\frac12(x\,dy-y\,dx).
\]

Then

\[
F=dA=dx\wedge dy.
\]

For the circle \(\gamma_a(t)=(a\cos 2\pi t,a\sin 2\pi t)\),

\[
\oint_{\gamma_a} A
=
\int_{D_a} dx\wedge dy
=
\pi a^2.
\]

Thus the holonomy angle is exactly \(\pi a^2\) for this chosen connection. This is not Gauss–Bonnet; it is Stokes’ theorem for an explicitly chosen connection.

For the non-Abelian prototype, define a connection on a trivial \(SO(3)\)-bundle with constant curvature components, for example

\[
F_{xy}=L_z,
\qquad
F_{yz}=L_x,
\qquad
F_{xz}=L_y,
\]

scaled appropriately. Then small loops in the \(xy\)-plane produce holonomy

\[
\operatorname{Hol}_{xy}(a)
=
\exp(\pi a^2 L_z),
\]

and the commutator signature follows from the \(so(3)\) bracket.

### 11. The fatigue correction is inserted by definition

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Equation (10) defines
  \[
  H_{\mathrm{corr}}(a)
  =
  H_{\mathrm{raw}}(a)
  -
  (0.5a^3+C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2})
  =
  \pi a^2.
  \]
- The correction is chosen so that the corrected quantity equals the geometric prediction.
- The stress-test section then uses
  \[
  \mu_F=a\kappa_V(a)+C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2}=0.0352,
  \]
  while Equation (10) uses \(0.5a\kappa_V(a)\).

Numerical check at \(a=0.3\):

\[
\kappa_V(a)=a^2=0.09,
\]

\[
a\kappa_V(a)=0.027,
\]

\[
0.5a\kappa_V(a)=0.0135,
\]

\[
C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2}=0.05(0.3)^{3/2}\approx 0.00822.
\]

Thus

\[
a\kappa_V+C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2}
\approx 0.03522,
\]

but

\[
0.5a\kappa_V+C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2}
\approx 0.02172.
\]

So the manuscript uses two different fatigue conventions.

**Required repair:**

1. Define raw holonomy before correction.
2. Estimate fatigue parameters from independent data or derive them from a specified fatigue dynamics.
3. Predict held-out loops without subtracting a fitted correction from the same loop.
4. Fix the coefficient convention: either use \(a\kappa_V\) or \(0.5a\kappa_V\), not both.
5. If the \(a^{3/2}\) exponent is not derived, replace it by an estimable exponent \(\beta\), or demote the \(3/2\) law to a conjecture.

A defensible fatigue model could be

\[
f_{k+1}
=
f_k
+
c_3 a^3
+
c_\beta a^\beta
+
\eta_k,
\]

with \(\beta\) derived or estimated separately. The corrected holonomy should then be a prediction, not a definition.

### 13. The empirical entropy is not a valid gauge-invariant entropy

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Definition 9.2 defines
  \[
  H_{\mathrm{emp}}(p_\gamma)
  =
  \log\sqrt{\det I(p_\gamma)}.
  \]

Mathematical status:

- This is a volume-density expression, not Shannon entropy.
- Under coordinate changes, \(\det I\) acquires Jacobian factors.
- On the simplex in redundant coordinates, the Fisher matrix is singular.
- Gauge invariance does not follow from Bregman invariance.

**Required repair:** use one of the following.

#### Coordinate-free Fisher volume ratio

Fix a reference measure \(\mu_0\). Define

\[
H_{\mathrm{emp}}(p)
=
\log
\frac{d\mu_F}{d\mu_0}(p),
\]

where

\[
d\mu_F=\sqrt{\det I(p)}\,dp
\]

in local minimal coordinates. This is invariant if \(\mu_0\) is a declared reference measure transforming correctly.

#### Fisher–Rao distance

Use

\[
d_{FR}(p,p_0)
=
2\arccos\left(\sum_i \sqrt{p_i p_{0,i}}\right)
\]

as a gauge-invariant observable.

#### Shannon entropy

If an entropy is needed, use

\[
H(p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i,
\]

which is a well-defined function on the simplex.

Do not call \(\log\sqrt{\det I(p)}\) an entropy without a reference measure and coordinate-free definition.

### 14. The quantum claims overreach

**Audit verdict: valid, with nuance.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Definition 2.6 defines a Liouvillian gap as
  \[
  \Delta=\min_{\lambda\ne 0}\operatorname{Re}(\lambda).
  \]
- Section 8/12 uses a Hamiltonian \(H=(\omega/2)\sigma_x\).
- It measures state change by trace distance and claims quadratic Zeno scaling.
- Proposition 12.2 describes Holevo information as mutual information between two states.

Mathematical status:

- For a closed Hamiltonian system, nonzero Liouvillian eigenvalues are imaginary, so a real-part gap is not the right object.
- The quantum Zeno theorem is usually about survival probability under repeated projection.
- Trace distance between a state and its small-time unitary evolution often scales linearly in \(\tau\), while survival-probability deficit scales quadratically.
- Holevo information is an ensemble quantity, not a mutual information between two individual density matrices.
- A CPTP channel does not automatically resolve classical self-reference.

**Required repair:**

Use a dissipative Lindbladian if invoking a Liouvillian gap:

\[
\mathcal L(\rho)
=
-i[H,\rho]
+
\sum_k
\left(
L_k\rho L_k^\dagger
-
\frac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}
\right),
\]

and define

\[
\Delta
=
\min_{\lambda\ne 0}
\{-\operatorname{Re}\lambda:\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal L)\}.
\]

For closed Hamiltonian Zeno scaling, use energy variance and survival probability.

Let \(P\) be a projection and \(\tau=t/N\). Then

\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\left(
P e^{-iHt/N}P
\right)^N
=
e^{-iPHPt}P.
\]

The survival probability satisfies

\[
p_N(t)
=
\left\|
\left(
P e^{-iHt/N}P
\right)^N
\psi
\right\|^2
=
1-\frac{t^2}{N}(\Delta H)^2+O(N^{-2}).
\]

Thus the survival deficit scales quadratically in the short-time interval. Use survival deficit or infidelity, not trace distance, for the exponent test.

For Holevo information, define an ensemble \(\{p_x,\rho_x\}\) and use

\[
\chi
=
S\left(\sum_x p_x\rho_x\right)
-
\sum_x p_x S(\rho_x).
\]

This bounds accessible classical information. In the commuting case it reduces to an ordinary classical information quantity after choosing the common eigenbasis, but it is not “mutual information between two states.”

To make the self-reference claim rigorous, define a quantum fixed-point equation such as

\[
\rho=\Phi(\rho),
\]

or a Zeno-projected equation

\[
\rho=P\Phi(P\rho P)P,
\]

and prove contraction or compactness. Zeno suppression alone does not imply endogenous self-reference.

### 15. The RAF inverse-limit construction is misnamed and unsupported

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Construction 14.1 says “inverse-limit construction” but then writes
  \[
  R_\infty=\operatorname{colim}_{\mathrm{dir}}R.
  \]
- It claims filtered colimits always exist in \(\mathrm{Optic}(\mathrm{Set})\) without proof.
- It claims viability preservation is inherited by the colimit without continuity assumptions.

Mathematical status:

- A filtered colimit is not an inverse limit.
- The maximal RAF obtained by union of a directed system of RAFs is a filtered colimit/direct limit, not an inverse limit.
- Colimits in an optic category require componentwise construction and compatibility.
- Viability preservation under colimit requires monotonicity/continuity of the viability functional.

**Required repair:**

Rename the construction:

\[
R_{\max}
=
\operatorname{colim}_{i\in I} R_i
=
\bigcup_i R_i,
\]

for a directed poset of RAFs ordered by inclusion.

If working in \(\mathrm{Optic}(\mathrm{Set})\), construct the colimit componentwise:

- forward carriers: colimit of forward carriers;
- backward carriers: limit or appropriate colimit depending on optic variance;
- residuals: colimit of residual objects;
- structure maps: induced by universal properties.

Then prove viability preservation under explicit assumptions:

If \(V(R)\) is monotone with respect to inclusion and continuous under directed unions, then

\[
V\left(\operatorname{colim}_i R_i\right)
=
\lim_i V(R_i).
\]

If \(V(R_i)\ge\varepsilon\) for all \(i\), then the colimit satisfies the same bound.

Without monotonicity/continuity, the claim is false.

### 16. The autopoiesis test is too weak and partly circular

**Audit verdict: valid.**

Manuscript evidence:

- Definition 3.5 says: remove a node \(m\), apply regeneration rules, and if \(m\) reappears, the system is autopoietic.

Mathematical/biological status:

- A node can reappear because of an external simulator rule, a hidden reset, or a trivial reinsertion operation.
- RAF closure is not equivalent to autopoiesis.
- Autopoiesis requires endogenous regeneration of processes, boundaries, and maintenance conditions.

# 5. What must be demoted to conjecture

Some claims are plausible but not currently provable from the manuscript’s assumptions. They should be demoted to precise conjectures rather than deleted.

---

## Conjecture 1: global stratified holonomy

For loops crossing constraint-switching boundaries, there exists a stratified connection with boundary transition maps such that holonomy is well-defined and satisfies a piecewise curvature formula.

Required ingredients:

- projected differential inclusion;
- viability-preserving reset maps;
- coherence conditions at triple intersections;
- a 2-categorical gluing theorem.

This is plausible but not proved.

## Conjecture 2: algorithmic curvature envelope

There exists an upper-semicomputable algorithmic viability curvature based on \(\operatorname{dist}_D\) that bounds all finite-code smooth surrogate curvatures up to machine-dependent constants.

This preserves the algorithmic-rate-distortion ambition without falsely differentiating Kolmogorov complexity.

## Conjecture 3: universal filtered colimits in optic categories

For suitable base categories \(\mathcal C\), the optic category \(\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C)\) admits filtered colimits constructed componentwise, and viability-preserving optics are closed under such colimits under monotonicity/continuity assumptions.

This is plausible but requires explicit categorical proof.

---

## Conjecture 4: heavy-tail \(3/2\) correction

The \(a^{3/2}\) fatigue correction arises from a stable heavy-tailed fluctuation process associated with path-ordered holonomy accumulation.

At present this is not derived from the Student-\(t\) assumption alone. Either derive it from a precise stable-process model or estimate the exponent.

## Conjecture 5: quantum self-reference resolution by Zeno contraction

A quantum predictor subject to frequent projective measurement admits a unique self-consistent state if the projected CPTP channel is a contraction.

This is highly plausible and can become a theorem once the channel, projection, and metric are specified.

# 7. Final assessment

The audit is essentially correct. The manuscript currently tries to make one object carry too many incompatible roles. The right move is not to abandon the ambition but to **factor the framework into typed layers with explicit interfaces**.

The strongest upgraded architecture is:

1. **Stratified Fisher-viability bundle** — rigorous geometry on constant-active-set strata.
2. **Connection and curvature** — genuine holonomy theorem.
3. **Viability-margin contraction** — separates hysteresis from fatality.
4. **Fisher-Weyl scale structure** — justifies \(CO(r)\) if scale is included.
5. **Smooth finite-code rate-distortion** — operationalizes algorithmic ideas without differentiating Kolmogorov complexity.
6. **Bregman-Hessian Noether theorem** — replaces the false Noether proposition.
7. **Typed optic composition plus realization functor** — preserves categorical unification without category errors.
8. **Explicit Banach contraction conditions** — turns numerical fixed-point evidence into a theorem when Lipschitz bounds are supplied.
9. **Filtered colimit of RAFs** — corrects inverse-limit misuse.
10. **Dynamical autopoiesis closure** — replaces weak node-reappearance test.
11. **Rigorous Zeno/CPTP fixed-point formulation** — preserves quantum ambition without overreach.

With these changes, the paper can honestly claim a broad, falsifiable, mathematically coherent framework rather than an overextended set of analogies.
