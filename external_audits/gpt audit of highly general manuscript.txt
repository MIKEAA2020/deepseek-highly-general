## Overall Assessment

The manuscript contains a potentially valuable core result:

> On a smooth constant-active-set stratum, constrained Fisher-minimal adaptation can define a transport law whose curvature predicts leading-order loop hysteresis.

That result is substantially narrower than the manuscript’s categorical, autopoietic, quantum, and algorithmic-rate-distortion claims. At present, the paper overstates what is proved, conflates several distinct mathematical objects, and reports numerical “confirmations” of quantities that are partly defined to make the predictions hold.

The central upgrade is to split the manuscript into separate theories with explicit interfaces:

1. stratified viability transport;
2. Fisher geometry and holonomy;
3. endogenous maintenance/autopoiesis;
4. RAF networks;
5. categorical composition;
6. quantum channels.

Only the first two currently form a coherent mathematical core.

## Critical Mathematical Flaws

### 1. The defined \(\kappa_V\) is not curvature

Definition 2.1 defines

\[
\kappa_V(\gamma)
=
\frac{1}{V_{\max}}
\int_0^1
(V_{\max}-V(\gamma(t)))\,dt,
\]

which is an average viability deficit. It has:

- no orientation;
- no dependence on alternative path order;
- no connection;
- no commutator;
- no 2-form structure;
- dependence on time parametrization unless the parametrization is fixed.

It is therefore not curvature. A flat connection can have nonzero value under this definition.

Later, Proposition 4.4 replaces it with a directional derivative of a Bregman divergence along a curvature 2-form. The manuscript never proves that the two definitions are equivalent. In the radial example they are simply different quantities that happen to be assigned related powers of \(a\).

**Required repair:** use separate notation:

\[
D_V(\gamma)
=
\frac{1}{|\gamma|}
\int_\gamma
(V_{\max}-V)\,ds
\]

for loop depth, and

\[
\kappa_V(u,v)
=
\max_\alpha
\frac{[-D h_\alpha(F(u,v))]_+}{h_\alpha}
\]

for viability-weighted curvature. Prove their relationship only where one exists.

---

### 2. The Fisher stabilizer is incorrectly identified as \(CO(n-1)\)

Chentsov’s theorem gives uniqueness of the Fisher metric under a class of statistical-map invariance requirements. It does not imply that the endogenous structure group of a policy system is

\[
CO(n-1)=\mathbb R_+\ltimes SO(n-1).
\]

The Fisher metric is preserved by isometries, not arbitrary positive scalings. The natural orthonormal frame bundle has group \(O(n-1)\), subject to the chosen manifold and frame-bundle construction. A reduction to \(SO(n-1)\) requires orientation. A reduction to \(CO(n-1)\) requires an explicitly defined conformal structure and conformal transport law.

The manuscript also alternates between \(O(n-1)\), \(SO(n-1)\), and \(CO(n-1)\) without specifying which one acts on which fiber.

**Required repair:** define the bundle, its transition maps, and the group action explicitly. Treat \(CO(n-1)\) as a modeling choice, not a consequence of Fisher geometry.

---

### 3. The proposed principal bundle is not constructed

Definition 3.1 gives

\[
\pi:\Delta^{n-1}\to\Theta,
\]

but a policy fiber bundle requires a total space \(E\), a projection

\[
\pi:E\to\Theta,
\]

and fibers \(E_\theta=\pi^{-1}(\theta)\). The displayed map has the wrong direction and does not specify a bundle.

Moreover, a principal \(G\)-bundle requires a free transitive \(G\)-action on each fiber. A policy simplex is not itself a principal \(CO(n-1)\)-space.

**Required repair:** define either:

- a policy bundle \(E\to\Theta\), with policy fibers;
- a frame bundle \(\mathrm{Fr}(E)\to E\), whose structure group is \(GL(n-1)\), \(O(n-1)\), or a declared reduction;
- or a connection on a trivial product bundle \(\Theta\times\mathcal P\).

The last option is probably sufficient and avoids unsupported bundle topology.

---

### 4. “Stratified connection” is only locally established

The Fisher-minimal constrained update is smooth only while the active constraint set and rank remain constant. At active-set transitions it is generally a projected differential inclusion or hybrid system.

The proposed 2-categorical span does not solve this problem. The manuscript explicitly says that the formal gluing, coherence, and well-defined holonomy are left for future work (pp. 4–5). Therefore the global SAVGS construction is not currently defined.

**Required repair:** state the main theorem only on constant-rank strata. For switching boundaries, provide one of:

- a projected dynamical system;
- a differential inclusion with a viability theorem;
- a stratified connection with proved transition maps;
- or a deliberate exclusion of switching loops.

Do not present the unfinished 2-categorical object as a completed framework.

---

### 5. Algorithmic rate-distortion is not a usable differentiable quantity

The quantity

\[
\mathrm{dist}_D(x)
=
\min\{|p|:U(p)=\hat x,\ d(x,\hat x)\le D\}
\]

is a shortest-program length, not a differentiable distance. It is:

- universal-machine dependent up to additive constants;
- generally uncomputable;
- discrete or discontinuous;
- undefined when no admissible output exists;
- unsuitable as the argument of a directional derivative without a relaxation.

The claim that it “eliminates the deterministic-versus-random coding gap” is misleading. It avoids the ensemble formulation by changing the problem. It does not establish a rate-distortion theorem or automatic computability.

Proposition 4.4 therefore differentiates a Bregman divergence whose argument is not a smooth state variable.

**Required repair:** choose one of:

1. a computable finite-model description length;
2. a normalized compression length;
3. a smooth surrogate \(r_D(x)\);
4. or retain Kolmogorov complexity as a theoretical quantity and remove it from the operational curvature formula.

If retained, the result must be an upper-semicomputable approximation statement, not an empirical single-trajectory observable.

---

### 6. The Bregman-Noether proposition is false as stated

Bregman divergences are not generally invariant under arbitrary affine transformations while keeping the same generator \(\phi\). The correct identity requires transforming the generator consistently:

\[
D_{\phi\circ A^{-1}}(Ap,Aq)=D_\phi(p,q)
\]

for an appropriate invertible affine map \(A\).

More importantly, Noether’s theorem requires a differentiable action functional or Lagrangian containing dynamical variables and their derivatives. The proposed

\[
L=D_\phi(d,\tilde d)+\lambda D_\phi(p,\tilde p)
\]

has no clearly specified time-dependent configuration space, velocity variables, boundary conditions, or action integral. Invariance of a scalar divergence does not automatically produce a conserved current.

The expression for \(J\) on p. 7 is consequently unsupported.

**Required repair:** either remove the Noether claim or formulate a genuine variational system:

\[
S[q]=\int L(q,\dot q,t)\,dt,
\]

define the group action on \(q\), prove \(L\)-invariance up to a total derivative, and derive the conserved quantity.

---

### 7. The optic-category theorem is a category error

The \(O_i\) in Construction 7.1 are described as individual optics, but Theorem 7.4 claims their composition is an endofunctor

\[
T:\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C)\to\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C).
\]

A composite optic is not automatically an endofunctor. To define an endofunctor, the manuscript must specify:

- the object map;
- the morphism map;
- preservation of identities;
- preservation of composition;
- the domain and codomain of every forward and backward map.

The condition \(A_i=M_{i+1}\) is not a sufficient categorical compatibility condition. The tables mix sets, CPTP maps, metric tensors, residuals, and numerical quantities as if they were objects of one category.

The proof of Theorem 7.4 proves only that composable optics can compose. It does not prove that an endofunctor exists, nor that it has a fixed point.

**Required repair:** choose one precise construction:

- a single optic in \(\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C)\);
- a functor between explicitly typed categories;
- a monoidal endofunctor with a fully specified action;
- or an ordinary composite update operator on a state space.

The optic language should be removed from the main theorem unless these data are supplied.

---

### 8. The Banach argument does not establish a fixed point

The numerical iteration of

\[
T_{\mathrm{reg}}(K)
=
(1-\lambda)T(K)+\lambda\Pi_K(T(K))
\]

does not prove that \(T\) is a contraction. Observed geometric convergence of finite trajectories is not a global Lipschitz bound.

Additional problems:

- \(T_{\mathrm{reg}}\) is not shown to map a complete metric space into itself;
- \(\Pi_K\) depends on the current set \(K\), so the operator is not clearly defined;
- a fixed point of \(T_{\mathrm{reg}}\) need not be a fixed point of \(T\);
- finite-dimensional sampled grids cannot establish contraction on an infinite or continuous state space;
- \(R^2\) of a log-linear tail fit is not a contraction proof.

The phrase “the unification object exists by Banach’s theorem” is therefore unjustified.

**Required repair:** prove an explicit bound

\[
d(Tx,Ty)\le q\,d(x,y),\qquad q<1
\]

on a specified complete metric space. If this cannot be proved, report numerical convergence only and call it numerical evidence for a fixed point of the regularized operator.

---

## Internal Inconsistencies

### 9. The prototype changes the meaning of \(n\)

At \(n=3\), the manuscript says the policy simplex is two-dimensional and the structure group is \(SO(2)\). Later it defines an \(n=3\) agent with a one-dimensional policy heading \(\theta\in S^1\). At \(n=4\), it retains a scalar heading but declares the structure group \(SO(3)\).

The dimension of the policy fiber, agent state, and structure group are not consistently related.

**Required repair:** use separate symbols:

- \(m\): simplex action count;
- \(d\): environmental parameter dimension;
- \(r\): policy-fiber dimension;
- \(n\): ambient state dimension.

Then define the structure group from the actual fiber geometry.

---

### 10. Holonomy, area, curvature, and Gauss–Bonnet are conflated

The identity

\[
H_{\mathrm{geo}}(a)=\pi a^2
\]

does not follow merely because the policy heading lies on \(S^1\). It requires a specified connection one-form whose curvature is a constant area form. A generic \(S^1\)-bundle connection does not have this property.

The phrase “Gauss–Bonnet collapse” is also inappropriate here: Gauss–Bonnet concerns integrated Gaussian curvature and topology of surfaces, not arbitrary policy connections.

The non-Abelian matrix commutator in Section 11 is also not automatically the curvature of the environmental connection. Group noncommutativity and base-space curvature must be separated.

---

### 11. The fatigue correction is inserted by definition

Equation (10) defines the corrected quantity so that

\[
H_{\mathrm{corr}}=H_{\mathrm{geo}}.
\]

This makes Claims C and E partly tautological. The value \(C_{\mathrm{fat}}=0.05\) is selected in advance, and the “observed” fit then recovers approximately the selected coefficient.

The manuscript needs to distinguish:

- a model-predicted raw observable;
- an independently estimated correction;
- a held-out prediction.

Subtracting the proposed correction before testing the prediction is not validation.

There is also a numerical inconsistency. With \(a=0.3\),

\[
a\kappa_V(a)=0.027,\qquad
0.5a\kappa_V(a)=0.0135,
\]

and

\[
0.05a^{3/2}\approx0.00822.
\]

Thus the stated \(\mu_F=0.0352\) uses \(a\kappa_V\), while Equation (10) uses \(0.5a\kappa_V\).

---

### 12. The control logic for Claim E is reversed

Definition 9.4 says that if the no-loop control agrees with the geometric prediction, the result is a common-cause artifact. If the control disagrees, the loop prediction is confirmed.

That is insufficient causal logic. A no-loop control disagreement only shows that the loop and no-loop conditions differ. It does not establish that curvature caused the difference.

A proper control requires matched exposure, matched resource consumption, shuffled ordering, frozen-learning conditions, and counterfactual transport predictions.

---

### 13. The empirical entropy is not a valid gauge-invariant entropy

\[
H_{\mathrm{emp}}=\log\sqrt{\det I(p_\gamma)}
\]

is not Shannon entropy. It is a coordinate-dependent volume-density expression unless a reference volume form is specified. On the simplex, the Fisher matrix in redundant coordinates is singular. Under coordinate changes, determinants acquire Jacobian factors.

The stated gauge invariance does not follow from Bregman invariance or the square-root embedding.

**Required repair:** use the Fisher–Rao distance or a coordinate-free volume ratio relative to a declared reference measure. Do not call the determinant expression entropy.

---

### 14. The quantum claims overreach

The Liouvillian definition

\[
\Delta=\min_{\lambda\ne0}\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)
\]

is problematic for a closed Hamiltonian system: its nonzero eigenvalues are generally imaginary, so the stated real-part gap is zero or otherwise unsuitable.

The Zeno result concerns survival probability under repeated projection. Trace distance between states need not scale quadratically in the same way. The observable, initial state, projector, and measurement channel must be specified precisely.

A quantum channel does not resolve the classical self-reference problem merely by being CPTP. Nor does Zeno suppression make a predictor endogenous.

Finally, Holevo information is defined from an ensemble or joint classical-quantum state. It is not generally “the mutual information between two states,” and commuting density matrices do not automatically make it the mutual information of the claimed predictor-output pair.

---

### 15. The RAF inverse-limit construction is misnamed and unsupported

The text alternates between “inverse limit” and

\[
R_\infty=\operatorname{colim}_{\mathrm{dir}}R.
\]

These are different constructions. A filtered colimit is not an inverse limit.

Finite limits in \(\mathcal C\) do not imply that \(\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C)\) has filtered colimits. In particular, the claim that filtered colimits “always exist” in \(\mathrm{Optic}(\mathrm{Set})\) requires proof and appropriate preservation conditions.

The statement that viability preservation is inherited by a colimit is also false without continuity or closure assumptions.

**Required repair:** define a genuine directed diagram, specify its maps, prove existence of the relevant limit or colimit, and prove preservation of RAF and viability properties separately.

---

### 16. The autopoiesis test is too weak and partly circular

Removing a graph node and checking whether it “reappears” does not establish autopoiesis. The node may be recreated by an external simulator, a hidden reset, or a rule that simply re-adds its label.

RAF closure is not equivalent to autopoietic organization. Autopoiesis requires endogenous regeneration of the processes and boundary conditions that sustain the network.

**Required repair:** add:

- degradation dynamics;
- internal material and energy flows;
- intervention on repair fluxes;
- external-supply controls;
- recovery and collapse trajectories;
- a positive-flux or strongly connected maintenance criterion.

## Empirical and Presentation Problems

The manuscript repeatedly says that all claims are “confirmed,” but the supplied text does not provide:

- source code;
- raw data;
- confidence intervals;
- preregistered thresholds;
- independent test sets;
- random seeds;
- uncertainty propagation;
- a definition of the actual agent dynamics;
- a derivation connecting the simulated observables to the formal objects.

Many reported fits appear generated from the same formulas used to define the observables. Exact-looking values such as \(R^2=1.0000\), machine precision, and preselected coefficients are not evidence of an empirical theory unless the simulation model is independently specified.

The heavy-tail section also does not support the claimed \(a^{3/2}\) law. A Student-\(t\) distribution with degrees of freedom \(1.5\) is not a generic derivation of a \(3/2\)-power correction. Tail index, stable-law scaling, finite-sample aggregation, and perturbation amplitude need to be mathematically connected.

## Profound Upgrades

### Upgrade 1: Make viability-weighted curvature the real central object

Define a smooth viability vector

\[
h=(h_1,\ldots,h_m),\qquad
\mathcal K=\{h_\alpha\ge0\}.
\]

On a constant active-set stratum, define Fisher-minimal transport by

\[
\dot p
=
-
G^{-1}J_p^\top
(J_pG^{-1}J_p^\top)^{-1}
J_\theta\dot\theta.
\]

Then define curvature \(F_{ij}\) from the horizontal lifts and contract it with viability covectors:

\[
\kappa_V
=
\sup_{\|u\wedge v\|=1}
\max_\alpha
\frac{[-D_ph_\alpha(F(u,v))]_+}{h_\alpha}.
\]

This has a clear interpretation: fractional viability-margin loss per unit environmental loop area.

---

### Upgrade 2: Separate three claims

The paper should distinguish:

1. **transport tangency:** the policy remains instantaneously constraint-compatible;
2. **holonomy:** closed environmental loops produce path-dependent policy drift;
3. **survival:** accumulated drift and ordinary disturbances do or do not exhaust the viability margin.

Only the second is fundamentally a curvature claim.

---

### Upgrade 3: Replace categorical unification with typed interfaces

If category theory is retained, define each domain as a category with explicit objects and morphisms:

- RAF networks and network homomorphisms;
- stochastic kernels and kernel morphisms;
- CPTP maps and channel intertwiners;
- policy bundles and connection-preserving maps.

Then define actual functors between these categories. A list of seven analogies is not an endofunctor.

---

### Upgrade 4: Use independent prediction for falsification

Estimate local transports from open-path experiments. Predict unseen loops by ordered composition. Do not fit the loop and then subtract a fitted correction.

Required controls include:

- reversed orientation;
- loop-size scaling;
- shuffled order;
- frozen-learning control;
- equal-exposure non-loop control;
- commuting perturbations;
- active-set-switching loops;
- external-repair knockout;
- no-holonomy baseline.

---

### Upgrade 5: Treat autopoiesis as a dynamical closure property

Use a maintenance graph with degradation and production fluxes. Test whether the indispensable maintenance subgraph has a positive internally supported steady state and whether knockouts cause predicted collapse or recovery.

This would convert the autopoiesis language from a label into an experimentally testable mechanism.

## Recommended Revised Contribution

The strongest publishable version would remove or demote the current claims about:

- \(CO(n-1)\) as a Fisher-derived structure group;
- algorithmic rate-distortion as an observable differentiable distance;
- the Bregman-Noether theorem;
- the categorical fixed-point theorem;
- the quantum resolution of self-reference;
- inverse-limit preservation of RAF viability.

The revised central theorem should be:

> On a smooth constant-active-set stratum, Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving adaptation defines a horizontal transport law. The curvature of this law predicts the leading-order policy holonomy around sufficiently small environmental loops. The survival consequence is determined separately by the contraction of that holonomy with viability-margin gradients, along-path disturbances, and endogenous maintenance capacity.

That claim is coherent, nontrivial, and falsifiable. The rest of the manuscript should be presented as possible extensions, not as already established consequences.
