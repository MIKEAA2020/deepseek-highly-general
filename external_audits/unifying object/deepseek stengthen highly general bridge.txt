## 1. Regge–Alexandrov curvature theorem for the FBA active‑set skeleton

**What it would establish**

The active‑set boundaries form a piecewise‑flat polyhedral complex. At each codim‑2 corner, the angle defect is already scale‑invariant and O(1) (M4b). In Regge calculus and Alexandrov geometry, this defect is *the* curvature of a piecewise‑flat metric.

A genuine theorem would be:

> Let \(\mathcal{C}\) be the active‑set complex of a nondegenerate parametric FBA. Define the discrete curvature measure \(\mu\) as the angle defect at each corner. Then the coarse‑grained measure \(\mu_\sigma = \mu * \phi_\sigma\) converges weakly, as \(\sigma \to 0\), to the curvature measure of a Riemannian metric \(g\) on the parameter space, and this \(g\) is the metric induced by the viability field \(V\).

If this can be proven, then the FBA curvature measure is no longer “like” curvature — it **is** the Regge–Alexandrov curvature of the viable parameter space. The geometric \(\kappa_V\) is its smooth refinement.

**Why this is genuine**  
It converts an analogy into a theorem. The only remaining question is whether the limiting Riemannian metric coincides with the manuscript’s \(g^{SAVGS}\). That is a well‑posed mathematical problem.

**Feasibility:** Medium. The local structure near regular crossings is controlled by parametric LP. The main difficulty is showing the angle defects satisfy a Bonnet–Myers or Alexandrov comparison condition as the complex is refined.

## 2. Weak convergence of coarse‑grained curvature measures to smooth curvature

**What it would establish**

M4c already proved the identity

\[
D^2(v * \phi_\sigma) = (D^2 v) * \phi_\sigma.
\]

Currently this is used to show a crossover. The next step is a convergence theorem:

> As the FBA polytope is successively refined (e.g., by adding reactions, constraints, or parameter grid resolution), the family of measures \(\mu_\sigma\) converges in Wasserstein or bounded‑Lipschitz distance to the curvature measure \(\kappa_{\text{geom}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g\).

This would prove that the smoothed FBA curvature is not just a convenient approximation; it is the **same object** in the limit.

**Why this is genuine**  
It provides the precise topology in which unification happens. Without such a theorem, “one measure, multiple resolutions” remains a description.

**Feasibility:** Medium‑high. The M4c identity is already exact. What remains is a stability estimate for the active‑set complex under refinement. Parametric LP stability results (e.g., Robinson, Klatte–Kummer) are likely sufficient.
