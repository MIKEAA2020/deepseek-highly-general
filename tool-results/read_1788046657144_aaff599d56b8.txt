    21→Introduction
    22→
    23→The notion that adaptation of a system to viability constraints can be unified across cognitive,
    24→biological, and physical domains has a long informal history in autopoiesis, viability theory [1],
    25→and information geometry [2, 3], but the precise categorical content of the unification has remained in flux. The dominant informal pattern is that viability- preserving systems, reflexively
    26→autocatalytic food-generated (RAF) reaction networks, predictive (Bayesian) belief updates, and
    27→quantum measurement schedules each admit a description in terms of a cost functional C whose
    28→stabilizer Stab(C) defines the structure group of the category of admissible updates. This pattern,
    29→however, has been expressed as a series of bridge-like correspondences without a single composition construction making the unification object a fixed point of a well-defined endofunctor.
    30→This article assembles seven arcs of prior domain-unification work into a single endofunctor
    31→T on the optic category Optic(C) of Riley [4] and Brunerie et al. [5], where C is taken to be a
    32→category with finite limits. The composition T = O7 ◦ · · · ◦ O1 of the seven optics is well-defined,
    33→associative, and unital; the unification object is the fixed point of T whenever T is contractive
    34→(Theorem 4.2).

   390→CPTP-Zeno Lift (Claim G)

   392→The recursive predictive-self-information (RPSI) paradox is unresolved in the classical Markov
   393→setting: an agent whose predictions are part of the state must compute its own predictive distribution, which requires its own predictive distribution, ad infinitum. The CPTP lift (Definition 2.5) resolves this by committing to a quantum- instantiated agent whose measurement
   394→schedule (τk , Pk )k≥1 is controllable. Under frequent measurement (τk → 0), the quantum Zeno
   395→effect [10, 11] (see also the standard reference [12] for the CPTP formalism) freezes the evolution
   396→inside the measurement subspace, breaking the predictive recursion.

   430→Theorem 4.2 reduces the existence of the unification object to the question of whether T is contractive on the operational state space X. The seven optics are implemented as continuous forward
   431→maps on X = [0, 1]d and iterated from compact starting subsets at five Bregman-regularization

   500→11 Discussion
   501→The single composition theorem (Theorem 4.2) consolidates seven arcs of prior domain-unification
   502→work into one endofunctor on Optic(C). The unification object becomes a fixed point of a welldefined endofunctor rather than a rhetorical construct; its existence reduces to a contractionrate question (Section 9), which is confirmed numerically across a 240-configuration robustness
   503→grid. The seven-claim falsification hierarchy (Definition 3.1) operationalizes the viability-weighted
   504→curvature κV as a falsifiable prediction at seven distinct levels, all confirmed within their stated
   505→tolerances in both the abelian (n = 3) and non-abelian (n = 4) regimes.
   506→The heavy-tail index stress test of Section 6 establishes that the Claim D suﬀicient condition
   507→is not a single-distribution artefact: the prediction reproduces at fconf = 1.000 across 200 MonteCarlo seeds at the operating scale, with a graceful breakdown at ση ≥ 5σop . The boundary maps
   508→cleanly onto the heavy-tail theory: the infinite-variance regime (df < 2) is broken only when the
   509→noise scale exceeds the deterministic mean by a factor of five.
   510→The n = 4 non-abelian generalization (Section 7) confirms that the viability-weighted curvature prediction is dimension- independent: the leading-order scaling κV (a) = a2 , the holonomy12
   511→
   512→area scaling c1 ∼ π, and the 32 -fatigue correction c2 ∼ Cfat persist without modification. The
   513→non-abelian structure contributes only higher-order
   514→√ corrections, captured by two independent falsifiable signatures: the commutator fit ccomm ∼ 2 π 2 in Claim C (Lemma 7.1, Proposition 7.2)
   515→and the non- commuting-sequence T -statistic in Claim E. The non-abelian signature is the so(3)
   516→commutation relation [lz , lx ] = ly .
   517→Limitations. (i) The numerical contraction argument (Section 9) is carried out in dimension
   518→d ≤ 20; the four WEAK configurations identified in Proposition 9.1 remain contractive (q < 1)
   519→with R2 < 0.9, but a sharper characterization of the tail-fit degradation at high Bregman strengths
   520→and high dimension is open. (ii) The CPTP-Zeno lift (Section 8) commits to a quantum- instantiated agent; the classical Markov setting remains unresolved. (iii) The inverse-limit construction
   521→(Section 10) is verified on a small reaction network; extension to larger networks is in principle
   522→straightforward (filtered colimits in Optic(Set) always exist) but unverified at scale.
   523→Future directions. (i) Structured noise expansion: replace the scalar Student–t noise in
   524→Claim D with matrix-valued so(3) noise under random generator directions, characterizing the
   525→non-abelian correction to the scalar bound. (ii) Tightening the four WEAK T-iteration configurations by exploring alternative Bregman divergences (e.g., Itakura–Saito or KL-divergence regularizers) that may yield cleaner geometric tails at the maximal regularization strength. (iii) The
   526→composition theorem (Theorem 4.2) is stated for C with finite limits; extension to ∞-categorical
   527→settings and homotopy type theory is open.
