     1→# 1. Verification of the audit points
     2→
     3→## Overall verdict
     4→
     5→The audit is **correct on essentially every major mathematical objection**. The manuscript contains a real and publishable core:
     6→
     7→> On a smooth constant-active-set stratum, Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving adaptation can define a transport law whose curvature predicts leading-order policy holonomy/hysteresis; survival consequences require a separate viability-margin analysis.
     8→
     9→The manuscript currently overreaches by identifying several distinct objects:
    10→
    11→- an average viability deficit with curvature;
    12→- Chentsov’s theorem with a \(CO(n-1)\) structure group;
    13→- a policy simplex with a principal bundle;
    14→- a composite optic with an endofunctor;
    15→- numerical convergence with Banach contraction;
    16→- a filtered colimit with an inverse limit;
    17→- a node-reappearance test with autopoiesis;
    18→- Kolmogorov/algorithmic rate-distortion with a smooth differentiable observable;
    19→- a scalar divergence invariance with Noether’s theorem;
    20→- trace-distance scaling with Zeno survival-probability scaling.
    21→
    22→None of these problems requires abandoning the broad program. They require **typing the objects, constructing the missing bundles/functors/metrics, separating curvature from viability depth, and replacing tautological validations with independent predictions**.

    28→### 1. The defined \(\kappa_V\) is not curvature
    29→
    30→**Audit verdict: valid.**
    31→
    32→Manuscript evidence:
    33→
    34→- Definition 2.1 defines  
    35→  \[
    36→  \kappa_V(\gamma_a)
    37→  =
    38→  \frac{1}{1}\int_0^1 (V_{\max}-V\circ\gamma_a(t))\,dt,
    39→  \]
    40→  which is an average viability deficit.
    41→- Proposition 4.4 later defines \(\kappa_V\) as a positive directional derivative of a Bregman divergence evaluated at an algorithmic rate-distortion distance.
    42→- No equivalence is proved.
    43→
    44→Mathematical status:
    45→
    46→- The Definition 2.1 object has no orientation, no connection, no commutator, no 2-form structure, and is parametrization-dependent unless arclength or constant-speed parametrization is fixed.
    47→- A flat connection can have nonzero average viability deficit.
    48→- Therefore Definition 2.1 is not curvature.
    49→
    50→**Required repair:** introduce two separate objects:
    51→
    52→\[
    53→D_V(\gamma)
    54→=
    55→\frac{1}{|\gamma|}
    56→\int_\gamma
    57→(V_{\max}-V)\,ds,
    58→\]
    59→
    60→a loop-depth or viability-deficit functional; and
    61→
    62→\[
    63→\kappa_V(u,v)
    64→=
    65→\max_\alpha
    66→\frac{[-\,d m_\alpha(F(u,v))]_+}{m_\alpha},
    67→\]
    68→
    69→a viability-weighted curvature functional built from a genuine curvature 2-form \(F\) and viability-margin functions \(m_\alpha\).
    70→
    71→The radial example may satisfy \(D_V(a)=a^2\) and \(H_{\mathrm{geo}}(a)=\pi a^2\), but that is a model-specific relation, not a definition of curvature.

    75→### 2. The Fisher stabilizer is incorrectly identified as \(CO(n-1)\)
    76→
    77→**Audit verdict: valid.**
    78→
    79→Manuscript evidence:
    80→
    81→- Definition 2.2 states that for the Fisher–Rao metric on the \(n\)-dimensional simplex, the stabilizer is  
    82→  \[
    83→  G_C=CO(n-1)=\mathbb R_+\ltimes SO(n-1).
    84→  \]
    85→- It attributes this to Chentsov’s theorem.
    86→
    87→Mathematical status:
    88→
    89→- Chentsov’s theorem gives uniqueness of the Fisher metric under statistical-map invariance.
    90→- It does not imply positive-scale invariance.
    91→- The Fisher metric is a Riemannian metric; its local orthonormal frame bundle has structure group \(O(n-1)\), or \(SO(n-1)\) after orientation.
    92→- A reduction to \(CO(n-1)\) requires an explicit conformal/scale structure.
    93→
    94→**Required repair:** state:
    95→
    96→\[
    97→G_{\mathrm{frame}}\subseteq O(r),
    98→\]
    99→
   100→for an \(r\)-dimensional policy fiber; if oriented,
   101→
   102→\[
   103→G_{\mathrm{frame}}\subseteq SO(r).
   104→\]
   105→
   106→If one adds an endogenous scale variable, e.g. effective precision, temperature, sample size, or Weyl factor, then one may define a conformal frame bundle with structure group
   107→
   108→\[
   109→CO(r)=\mathbb R_+\ltimes SO(r).
   110→\]
   111→
   112→But \(CO(r)\) must be a declared modeling structure, not a consequence of Chentsov alone.

   123→  \[
   124→  \pi:\Delta^{n-1}\to\Theta.
   125→  \]

   127→  \[
   128→  \pi:E\to\Theta
   129→  \]

   131→
   132→Mathematical status:
   133→
   134→- A policy simplex is not automatically a principal \(CO(n-1)\)-space.
   135→- A principal \(G\)-bundle requires a free transitive \(G\)-action on each fiber.
   136→- The manuscript does not define total space, transition functions, or group action.
   137→

   139→
   140→The simplest is a trivial policy bundle:
   141→
   142→\[
   143→E=\Theta\times \mathcal P,
   144→\qquad
   145→\pi(\theta,p)=\theta,
   146→\]
   147→
   148→where \(\mathcal P\) is the policy manifold, e.g. the open simplex \(\Delta^{m-1}_\circ\), \(S^1\), \(SO(3)\), or a reduced policy space.
   149→

   151→
   152→1. the orthonormal frame bundle of the vertical Fisher metric:
   153→   \[

   155→   \]
   156→   with structure group \(O(r)\) or \(SO(r)\);
   157→
   158→2. a conformal frame bundle with structure group \(CO(r)\), after introducing a scale bundle;

   162→   P=\Theta\times G\to\Theta

   199→### 5. Algorithmic rate-distortion is not a usable differentiable quantity
   200→
   201→**Audit verdict: valid.**
   202→
   203→Manuscript evidence:
   204→
   205→- Definition 4.1 defines
   206→  \[
   207→  \operatorname{dist}_D(x)
   208→  =
   209→  \min\{|p|:U(p)=\hat x,\ d(x,\hat x)\le D\}.
   210→  \]
   211→- Proposition 4.4 differentiates a Bregman divergence evaluated at this quantity.
   212→
   213→Mathematical status:
   214→
   215→- \(\operatorname{dist}_D(x)\) is a shortest-program length.
   216→- It is machine-dependent up to additive constants.
   217→- It is generally uncomputable.
   218→- It is integer-valued or discrete.
   219→- It is not a smooth state variable.
   220→- It cannot be inserted into a directional derivative without regularization or smoothing.
   221→
   222→The claim that it “eliminates the deterministic-versus-random coding gap” is only partly meaningful: it changes the object from ensemble rate-distortion to single-string description length, but it does not produce a differentiable operational curvature.
   223→
   224→**Required repair:** choose one of the following.
   225→
   226→#### Option A: finite-code description length
   227→
   228→Use a finite, computable code family and define
   229→
   230→\[
   231→L_D^N(x)
   232→=
   233→\min\{\ell(c): c\in C_N,\ \operatorname{dec}(c)=\hat x,\ d(x,\hat x)\le D\}.
   234→\]
   235→
   236→This is still discrete but computable.
   237→
   238→#### Option B: smooth surrogate
   239→
   240→Define a softened rate-distortion potential, for example
   241→
   242→\[
   243→r_{\tau,\beta,D}(x)
   244→=
   245→-\tau
   246→\log
   247→\sum_{c}
   248→2^{-\ell(c)/\tau}
   249→\exp\left(
   250→-\beta [d(x,\operatorname{dec}(c))-D]_+^2/\tau
   251→\right).
   252→\]
   253→
   254→For \(\tau>0\), under smooth decoder/distortion assumptions, this is \(C^2\) or smoother. Then define
   255→
   256→\[
   257→h_\alpha(\theta,x)
   258→=
   259→D_\phi(r_{\tau,\beta,D}(x),r_{\tau,\beta,D}(x_0)).
   260→\]
   261→
   262→Now Proposition 4.4-style curvature formulas are meaningful.
   263→
   264→#### Option C: keep Kolmogorov complexity theoretically
   265→
   266→Retain \(\operatorname{dist}_D\) only as an upper-semicomputable theoretical bound, not as an empirical single-trajectory differentiable observable.
   267→
   268→**Elevated path:** use Option B for operational theorems and Option C for asymptotic upper bounds. The algorithmic version becomes a conjectural upper envelope unless a smooth envelope theorem is proved.

   272→### 6. The Bregman-Noether proposition is false as stated
   273→
   274→**Audit verdict: valid.**
   275→
   276→Manuscript evidence:
   277→
   278→- Proposition 5.1 claims that because a Bregman divergence is invariant under a one-parameter affine transformation, Noether’s theorem yields a conserved current.
   279→- The Lagrangian
   280→  \[
   281→  L=D_\phi(d,\tilde d)+\lambda D_\phi(p,\tilde p)
   282→  \]
   283→  has no configuration space, velocities, boundary conditions, or action integral.
   284→
   285→Mathematical status:
   286→
   287→- Noether’s theorem applies to differentiable actions on a configuration space with a Lagrangian depending on trajectories and velocities.
   288→- Invariance of a scalar divergence under coordinate transformation does not by itself create a conserved current.
   289→- Bregman divergences are not invariant under arbitrary affine transformations while holding the generator fixed; the generator must transform appropriately.
   290→
   291→**Required repair:** replace Proposition 5.1 with a genuine symmetry-momentum theorem.
   292→
   293→Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^r\) be open convex, \(\phi\in C^3\) strictly convex, and
   294→
   295→\[
   296→g_\phi(q)=\nabla^2\phi(q)
   297→\]
   298→
   299→the Hessian metric. Consider the geodesic Lagrangian
   300→
   301→\[
   302→L(q,\dot q)
   303→=
   304→\frac12 g_\phi(q)(\dot q,\dot q)-U(q).
   305→\]
   306→
   307→Let \(\xi\) be a vector field generating affine transformations preserving the Hessian metric:
   308→
   309→\[
   310→\mathcal L_\xi g_\phi=0,
   311→\]
   312→
   313→and preserving the potential:
   314→
   315→\[
   316→\xi(U)=0.
   317→\]
   318→
   319→Then Noether’s theorem gives the conserved momentum
   320→
   321→\[
   322→J_\xi(q,\dot q)
   323→=
   324→g_\phi(q)(\dot q,\xi(q)).
   325→\]
   326→
   327→If the affine flow preserves \(\phi\) only up to an affine function,
   328→
   329→\[
   330→\phi\circ g_t=\phi+\ell_t,
   331→\]
   332→
   333→then the Bregman divergence is invariant in the appropriate paired sense, and the same symmetry argument applies to the Hessian metric.
   334→
   335→This supplies a valid replacement for the “Bregman-Noether correspondence”: not divergence invariance alone, but affine isometries of the Hessian metric yield conserved momenta.
   336→

   339→### 7. The optic-category theorem is a category error
   340→
   341→**Audit verdict: valid.**
   342→
   343→Manuscript evidence:
   344→
   345→- Construction 7.1 lists seven optics \(O_1,\dots,O_7\).
   346→- Theorem 7.4 claims that their composition is an endofunctor
   347→  \[
   348→  T:\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C)\to\mathrm{Optic}(\mathcal C).
   349→  \]
   350→- The proof only proves associativity of optic composition.
   351→
   352→Mathematical status:
   353→
   354→- A composite optic is an optic, not automatically an endofunctor.
   355→- To define an endofunctor one needs an object map, morphism map, identity preservation, and composition preservation.
   356→- The compatibility condition \(A_i=M_{i+1}\) is not sufficient to define a functor on the entire optic category.
   357→- The table mixes sets, CPTP maps, metric tensors, residuals, and scalars without a common typing discipline.
   358→
   359→**Required repair:** separate three notions.
   360→
   361→#### Correct minimal theorem: composite endo-optic
   362→
   363→Let \(\mathcal I\) be a typed interface category with objects
   364→
   365→\[
   366→I_0,I_1,\dots,I_7,
   367→\]
   368→
   369→and an interface isomorphism
   370→
   371→\[
   372→\sigma:I_7\cong I_0.
   373→\]
   374→
   375→Let
   376→
   377→\[
   378→O_i:I_{i-1}\rightleftarrows I_i
   379→\]
   380→
   381→be typed optics. Then
   382→
   383→\[
   384→O
   385→=
   386→\sigma\circ O_7\circ\cdots\circ O_1
   387→\]
   388→
   389→is a well-defined endo-optic on \(I_0\).
   390→
   391→This is true by associativity and unitality of optic composition.
   392→
   393→#### Stronger valid theorem: endofunctor on a category of periodic systems
   394→
   395→Define a category \(\mathsf{Sys}_7\) of seven-periodic typed systems. An object is a tuple
   396→
   397→\[
   398→(X_1,\dots,X_7,f_1,\dots,f_7),
   399→\]
   400→
   401→where \(f_i:X_i\to X_{i+1}\), with \(X_8=X_1\). A morphism is a compatible family of maps.
   402→
   403→If each arc \(O_i\) is lifted to a functor
   404→
   405→\[
   406→F_i:\mathsf{Sys}_i\to\mathsf{Sys}_{i+1},
   407→\]
   408→
   409→then the composite
   410→
   411→\[
   412→\mathcal T=F_7\circ\cdots\circ F_1
   413→\]
   414→
   415→is an endofunctor on a suitably identified periodic category. Its fixed point, if it exists, is a coalgebra
   416→
   417→\[
   418→X\cong \mathcal T(X).
   419→\]
   420→
   421→This preserves the ambition but requires explicit functorial semantics.
   422→
   423→#### Operational fixed point theorem
   424→
   425→Alternatively, let \(R\) be a realization functor from endo-optics to endomaps on a complete metric space \(S\). If
   426→
   427→\[
   428→T=R(O):S\to S
   429→\]
   430→
   431→is a contraction, then \(T\) has a unique fixed point by Banach’s theorem.
   432→
   433→This is the clean way to keep the fixed-point claim without claiming that an optic is automatically an endofunctor.
   434→

   437→### 8. The Banach argument does not establish a fixed point
   438→
   439→**Audit verdict: valid.**
   440→
   441→Manuscript evidence:
   442→
   443→- Section 13 reports numerical convergence of
   444→  \[
   445→  T_{\mathrm{reg}}(K)
   446→  =
   447→  (1-\lambda)T(K)+\lambda\Pi_K(T(K)).
   448→  \]
   449→- It concludes that “the unification object exists by Banach’s theorem.”
   450→- No global Lipschitz bound is proved.
   451→
   452→Mathematical status:
   453→
   454→- Observed geometric convergence is not a contraction proof.
   455→- \(R^2\) of a tail fit is not a Lipschitz constant.
   456→- \(\Pi_K\) depending on the current set \(K\) makes the operator definition suspect.
   457→- A fixed point of the regularized operator need not be a fixed point of the original operator.
   458→- Finite grids do not prove contraction on continuous spaces.
   459→
   460→**Required repair:** prove an explicit bound
   461→
   462→\[
   463→d(Tx,Ty)\le q d(x,y),
   464→\qquad q<1,
   465→\]
   466→
   467→on a complete metric space.
   468→
   469→A clean sufficient theorem is:
   470→
   471→Let \(X=[0,1]^d\) with Euclidean metric. Let
   472→
   473→\[
   474→f=f_7\circ\cdots\circ f_1:X\to X
   475→\]
   476→
   477→with each \(f_i\) Lipschitz and
   478→
   479→\[
   480→L=\prod_{i=1}^7 \operatorname{Lip}(f_i)<1.
   481→\]
   482→
   483→Let \(C\subset X\) be closed and convex, and let \(\Pi_C\) be the Euclidean projection. Define
   484→
   485→\[
   486→T_\lambda(x)
   487→=
   488→(1-\lambda)f(x)+\lambda \Pi_C(f(x)),
   489→\qquad
   490→0\le\lambda<1.
   491→\]
   492→
   493→Then
   494→
   495→\[
   496→\operatorname{Lip}(T_\lambda)\le L<1,
   497→\]
   498→
   499→because \(\Pi_C\) is nonexpansive. Therefore \(T_\lambda\) has a unique fixed point.
   500→
   501→If the manuscript cannot prove \(L<1\), it should say:
   502→
   503→> Numerical evidence suggests contraction of the regularized update, but Banach existence has not been proved.
   504→
   505→To elevate the claim, provide analytic Jacobian bounds or Lipschitz constants for each optic realization.

   544→### 10. Holonomy, area, curvature, and Gauss–Bonnet are conflated
   545→
   546→**Audit verdict: valid.**
   547→
   548→Manuscript evidence:
   549→
   550→- Remark 9.1 states that \(H_{\mathrm{geo}}(a)=\pi a^2\) is the loop area and calls the equality a “Gauss–Bonnet collapse.”
   551→- But no connection one-form is explicitly constructed.
   552→- Gauss–Bonnet concerns integrated Gaussian curvature and topology, not an arbitrary policy connection.
   553→
   554→Mathematical status:
   555→
   556→- The area law \(H=\pi a^2\) follows only if one specifies a connection whose curvature is the standard area form.
   557→- A generic \(S^1\)-bundle connection need not have this property.
   558→- Noncommutativity of \(SO(3)\) is not the same as base-space curvature; matrix commutators and curvature 2-forms must be related explicitly.
   559→
   560→**Required repair:** construct the connection.
   561→
   562→For the abelian prototype, take
   563→
   564→\[
   565→B=\mathbb R^2,
   566→\qquad
   567→P=S^1,
   568→\qquad
   569→E=B\times S^1.
   570→\]
   571→
   572→Let \(\psi\) be the policy heading. Define the connection one-form
   573→
   574→\[
   575→\alpha=d\psi + A,
   576→\]
   577→
   578→with
   579→
   580→\[
   581→A=\frac12(x\,dy-y\,dx).
   582→\]
   583→
   584→Then
   585→
   586→\[
   587→F=dA=dx\wedge dy.
   588→\]
   589→
   590→For the circle \(\gamma_a(t)=(a\cos 2\pi t,a\sin 2\pi t)\),
   591→
   592→\[
   593→\oint_{\gamma_a} A
   594→=
   595→\int_{D_a} dx\wedge dy
   596→=
   597→\pi a^2.
   598→\]
   599→
   600→Thus the holonomy angle is exactly \(\pi a^2\) for this chosen connection. This is not Gauss–Bonnet; it is Stokes’ theorem for an explicitly chosen connection.
   601→
   602→For the non-Abelian prototype, define a connection on a trivial \(SO(3)\)-bundle with constant curvature components, for example
   603→
   604→\[
   605→F_{xy}=L_z,
   606→\qquad
   607→F_{yz}=L_x,
   608→\qquad
   609→F_{xz}=L_y,
   610→\]
   611→
   612→scaled appropriately. Then small loops in the \(xy\)-plane produce holonomy
   613→
   614→\[
   615→\operatorname{Hol}_{xy}(a)
   616→=
   617→\exp(\pi a^2 L_z),
   618→\]
   619→
   620→and the commutator signature follows from the \(so(3)\) bracket.

   624→### 11. The fatigue correction is inserted by definition
   625→
   626→**Audit verdict: valid.**
   627→
   628→Manuscript evidence:
   629→
   630→- Equation (10) defines
   631→  \[
   632→  H_{\mathrm{corr}}(a)
   633→  =
   634→  H_{\mathrm{raw}}(a)
   635→  -
   636→  (0.5a^3+C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2})
   637→  =
   638→  \pi a^2.
   639→  \]
   640→- The correction is chosen so that the corrected quantity equals the geometric prediction.
   641→- The stress-test section then uses
   642→  \[
   643→  \mu_F=a\kappa_V(a)+C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2}=0.0352,
   644→  \]
   645→  while Equation (10) uses \(0.5a\kappa_V(a)\).
   646→
   647→Numerical check at \(a=0.3\):
   648→
   649→\[
   650→\kappa_V(a)=a^2=0.09,
   651→\]
   652→
   653→\[
   654→a\kappa_V(a)=0.027,
   655→\]
   656→
   657→\[
   658→0.5a\kappa_V(a)=0.0135,
   659→\]
   660→
   661→\[
   662→C_{\mathrm{fat}}a^{3/2}=0.05(0.3)^{3/2}\approx 0.00822.
   663→\]
   664→
   665→Thus
   666→

   808→### 14. The quantum claims overreach
   809→
   810→**Audit verdict: valid, with nuance.**
   811→
   812→Manuscript evidence:
   813→
   814→- Definition 2.6 defines a Liouvillian gap as
   815→  \[
   816→  \Delta=\min_{\lambda\ne 0}\operatorname{Re}(\lambda).
   817→  \]
   818→- Section 8/12 uses a Hamiltonian \(H=(\omega/2)\sigma_x\).
   819→- It measures state change by trace distance and claims quadratic Zeno scaling.
   820→- Proposition 12.2 describes Holevo information as mutual information between two states.
   821→
   822→Mathematical status:
   823→
   824→- For a closed Hamiltonian system, nonzero Liouvillian eigenvalues are imaginary, so a real-part gap is not the right object.
   825→- The quantum Zeno theorem is usually about survival probability under repeated projection.
   826→- Trace distance between a state and its small-time unitary evolution often scales linearly in \(\tau\), while survival-probability deficit scales quadratically.
   827→- Holevo information is an ensemble quantity, not a mutual information between two individual density matrices.
   828→- A CPTP channel does not automatically resolve classical self-reference.
   829→
   830→**Required repair:**
   831→
   832→Use a dissipative Lindbladian if invoking a Liouvillian gap:
   833→
   834→\[
   835→\mathcal L(\rho)
   836→=
   837→-i[H,\rho]
   838→+
   839→\sum_k
   840→\left(
   841→L_k\rho L_k^\dagger
   842→-
   843→\frac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}
   844→\right),
   845→\]
   846→
   847→and define
   848→
   849→\[
   850→\Delta
   851→=
   852→\min_{\lambda\ne 0}
   853→\{-\operatorname{Re}\lambda:\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal L)\}.
   854→\]
   855→
   856→For closed Hamiltonian Zeno scaling, use energy variance and survival probability.
   857→
   858→Let \(P\) be a projection and \(\tau=t/N\). Then
   859→
   860→\[
   861→\lim_{N\to\infty}
   862→\left(
   863→P e^{-iHt/N}P
   864→\right)^N
   865→=
   866→e^{-iPHPt}P.
   867→\]
   868→
   869→The survival probability satisfies
   870→
   871→\[
   872→p_N(t)
   873→=
   874→\left\|
   875→\left(
   876→P e^{-iHt/N}P
   877→\right)^N
   878→\psi
   879→\right\|^2
   880→=
   881→1-\frac{t^2}{N}(\Delta H)^2+O(N^{-2}).
   882→\]
   883→
   884→Thus the survival deficit scales quadratically in the short-time interval. Use survival deficit or infidelity, not trace distance, for the exponent test.
   885→
   886→For Holevo information, define an ensemble \(\{p_x,\rho_x\}\) and use
   887→
   888→\[
   889→\chi
   890→=
   891→S\left(\sum_x p_x\rho_x\right)
   892→-
   893→\sum_x p_x S(\rho_x).
   894→\]
   895→
   896→This bounds accessible classical information. In the commuting case it reduces to an ordinary classical information quantity after choosing the common eigenbasis, but it is not “mutual information between two states.”
   897→
   898→To make the self-reference claim rigorous, define a quantum fixed-point equation such as
   899→
   900→\[
   901→\rho=\Phi(\rho),
   902→\]
   903→
   904→or a Zeno-projected equation
   905→
   906→\[
   907→\rho=P\Phi(P\rho P)P,
   908→\]
   909→
   910→and prove contraction or compactness. Zeno suppression alone does not imply endogenous self-reference.

  1102→## 2.3 Fisher-minimal constrained transport as a connection
  1103→
  1104→Assume the policy fiber \(\mathcal P\) carries the Fisher–Rao metric \(g^F\). For the open simplex \(\Delta^{m-1}_\circ\), use the square-root embedding
  1105→
  1106→\[
  1107→\psi_i(p)=2\sqrt{p_i},
  1108→\]
  1109→
  1110→so that
  1111→
  1112→\[
  1113→d_{FR}(p,q)
  1114→=
  1115→2\arccos\left(\sum_i \sqrt{p_iq_i}\right).
  1116→\]
  1117→
  1118→On a constant-active-set stratum \(S_A\), write local coordinates \((\theta,p)\), where \(\theta\in B\) and \(p\in\mathcal P\).
  1119→
  1120→Let active constraints be
  1121→
  1122→\[
  1123→h_A(\theta,p)=0.
  1124→\]
  1125→
  1126→Given a base velocity \(\dot\theta\), choose the vertical policy velocity \(\dot p\) minimizing Fisher kinetic energy
  1127→
  1128→\[
  1129→\frac12 \dot p^\top G(p)\dot p
  1130→\]
  1131→
  1132→subject to the linearized constraint
  1133→
  1134→\[
  1135→J_p\dot p+J_\theta\dot\theta=0,
  1136→\]
  1137→
  1138→where
  1139→
  1140→\[
  1141→J_p=D_p h_A,
  1142→\qquad
  1143→J_\theta=D_\theta h_A,
  1144→\]
  1145→
  1146→and \(G\) is the Fisher metric matrix.
  1147→
  1148→If
  1149→
  1150→\[
  1151→M=J_pG^{-1}J_p^\top
  1152→\]
  1153→
  1154→is invertible, the Fisher-minimal horizontal lift is
  1155→
  1156→\[
  1157→\dot p
  1158→=
  1159→-G^{-1}J_p^\top
  1160→\left(
  1161→J_pG^{-1}J_p^\top
  1162→\right)^{-1}
  1163→J_\theta\dot\theta.
  1164→\]

  1169→

  1171→\omega
  1172→=
  1173→dp
  1174→+
  1175→G^{-1}J_p^\top
  1176→\left(
  1177→J_pG^{-1}J_p^\top

  1183→
  1184→\[
  1185→\Omega
  1186→=
  1187→d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega,

  1190→with the wedge term present only in non-Abelian frame formulations.
