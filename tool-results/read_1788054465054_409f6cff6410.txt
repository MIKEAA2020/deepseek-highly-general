    84→\small
    85→\textbf{Abstract.} A single categorical construction is developed that
    86→unifies viability-constrained adaptation, reflexively autocatalytic food-
    87→generated (RAF) reaction networks, the Fisher--Rao information-geometric
    88→structure of belief updates, completely-positive trace-preserving (CPTP)
    89→quantum channels with Zeno-type measurement schedules, and non-abelian
    90→holonomy of policy loops under the structure group $\CO(n-1)$. The
    91→construction rests on the Stratified Autopoietic Viability Geometric
    92→System (SAVGS), a five-component object (continuous control manifold,
    93→policy fiber, open probability simplex, strict viability margin,
    94→endogenous maintenance graph) whose geometry is a stratified principal
    95→$\CO(n-1)$-bundle; the viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ is derived as
    96→the positive part of the directional derivative of a Bregman divergence
    97→evaluated at the algorithmic rate-distortion distance, an intrinsically
    98→single-string quantity that eliminates the deterministic-versus-random-
    99→coding gap of classical rate-distortion theory. The construction
   100→assembles seven prior arcs of domain unification into one endofunctor
   101→on the optic category $\Optic(\CC)$ of Riley; the resulting composition
   102→theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}) makes the unification object a
   103→well-defined fixed point whose existence reduces to an empirical
   104→contraction-rate question, decoupled from definitional rhetoric. A
   105→Bregman-divergence Noether correspondence (Proposition~\ref{prop:noether})
   106→supplies the affine-invariant structure required for the gauge
   107→invariance of $\kk$. A seven-claim falsification hierarchy
   108→(Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy}) is operationalized in the minimal
   109→$n=3$ prototype (abelian, $\so(2)$) and in the $n=4$ non-abelian
   110→prototype ($\so(3)$, $[\mathfrak{l}_z,
   111→\mathfrak{l}_x]=\mathfrak{l}_y$); the heavy-tail index of the fatigue
   112→noise is stress-tested across the Student--$t$ tail-parameter axis. The
   113→main proposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:main}) states that on smooth
   114→constant-active-set strata, Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving
   115→adaptation defines a stratified connection whose viability-weighted
   116→curvature predicts leading-order policy hysteresis. All seven claims
   117→are confirmed within their stated tolerances.

   131→\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
   132→% =============================================================
   133→
   134→The notion that adaptation of a system to viability constraints can be
   135→unified across cognitive, biological, and physical domains has a long
   136→informal history in autopoiesis, viability theory~\cite{aubin2011}, and
   137→information geometry~\cite{chentsov1982,amari2000}, but the precise
   138→categorical content of the unification has remained in flux. The dominant informal pattern is that viability-
   139→preserving systems, reflexively autocatalytic food-generated (RAF)
   140→reaction networks, predictive (Bayesian) belief updates, and quantum
   141→measurement schedules each admit a description in terms of a cost
   142→functional $C$ whose stabilizer $\Stab(C)$ defines the structure group of
   143→the category of admissible updates. This pattern, however, has been
   144→expressed as a series of bridge-like correspondences without a single
   145→composition construction making the unification object a fixed point of
   146→a well-defined endofunctor.
   147→
   148→This article assembles seven arcs of prior domain-unification work into
   149→a single endofunctor $T$ on the optic category $\Optic(\CC)$ of
   150→Riley~\cite{riley2018optics} and Brunerie et al.~\cite{brunerie2020},
   151→where $\CC$ is taken to be a category with finite limits. The composition
   152→$T = \OO_7 \circ \cdots \circ \OO_1$ of the seven optics is
   153→well-defined, associative, and unital; the unification object is the
   154→fixed point of $T$ whenever $T$ is contractive (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}).
   155→The framework rests on the Stratified Autopoietic Viability Geometric
   156→System (SAVGS, Section~\ref{sec:savgs}), a five-component object whose
   157→geometry is a stratified principal $\CO(n-1)$-bundle; on this object,
   158→the viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ is well-defined, gauge-invariant,
   159→and falsifiable.
   160→
   161→The viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ is the leading-order operational
   162→form of the cost functional $C$, defined on policy loops in the agent
   163→parameter space. It is constructed (Section~\ref{sec:ard}) as the
   164→positive part of the directional derivative of a Bregman divergence
   165→$h_\alpha$ evaluated at the algorithmic rate-distortion distance
   166→$\distD(x)$, a single-string quantity that eliminates the
   167→deterministic-versus-random-coding gap of classical rate-distortion
   168→theory. For a circular loop $\gamma_a$ of amplitude $a$ in a radially
   169→symmetric viability landscape $V$ with maximum $V_{\max}$ at the origin,
   170→the operational form reduces to
   171→\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappaV}
   172→  \kk(a) \;=\; \frac{1}{V_{\max}}\,\langle V_{\max} - V\circ\gamma_a\rangle
   173→  \;=\; a^2,
   174→\end{equation}
   175→independent of dimension; the structure group $\Stab(\kk)$ is then the
   176→endogenous structure group of the system and is computed, not chosen.
   177→
   178→A Bregman-divergence Noether correspondence
   179→(Section~\ref{sec:noether}, Proposition~\ref{prop:noether}) supplies
   180→the affine-invariant structure required for the gauge invariance of
   181→$\kk$: under a one-parameter affine transformation on the dual
   182→coordinate, the Bregman divergence is invariant, and Noether's theorem
   183→yields a conserved current. The correspondence is a theorem with
   184→explicit, checkable preconditions: the distortion measure and the
   185→source prior must both be Bregman divergences. A system that violates
   186→this precondition refutes the correspondence.
   187→
   188→The principal contributions are summarized as follows.
   189→
   190→\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
   191→\item The SAVGS framework (Definition~\ref{def:savgs}), a
   192→five-component stratified object that resolves the type confusions,
   193→gauge ambiguities, and missing premises of prior formulations. The
   194→geometry is a stratified principal $\CO(n-1)$-bundle, and $\kk$ is
   195→computed on this object.
   196→\item A single composition theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}) for
   197→the seven optics in $\Optic(\CC)$, replacing the rhetorical
   198→``bridge-rung'' construction of prior work. The unification object is
   199→the fixed point of $T$, well-defined up to canonical isomorphism
   200→whenever $T$ is contractive.
   201→\item The algorithmic rate-distortion distance $\distD$
   202→(Definition~\ref{def:ard}) and the derivation of $\kk$ from a Bregman
   203→divergence evaluated at $\distD$
   204→(Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-derivation}), supplying the deterministic
   205→single-string content required for the per-trajectory falsification
   206→protocol.
   207→\item A Bregman-divergence Noether correspondence
   208→(Proposition~\ref{prop:noether}) that turns the prior Noether analogy
   209→into a theorem with checkable preconditions.
   210→\item A seven-claim falsification hierarchy
   211→(Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy}) on the operational form $\kk$, with
   212→five derivative claims (A--E) in the $n=3$ prototype, an $n\geq 4$
   213→non-abelian extension (Claim F), and a CPTP-Zeno quantum lift
   214→(Claim~G).
   215→\item Numerical confirmation of all seven claims within their stated
   216→tolerances, including heavy-tail index stress-testing of Claim~D across
   217→the Student--$t$ tail-parameter axis (Section~\ref{sec:stress}) and
   218→generalization of A--E to the $n=4$ non-abelian regime
   219→(Section~\ref{sec:n4}).
   220→\item An inverse-limit construction of the directed system of RAFs in
   221→$\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$, yielding the maximal RAF $R_{\max}$ as the
   222→colimit (Construction~\ref{con:invlim}).
   223→\item A numerical Banach contraction argument for $T$ over $X=[0,1]^d$
   224→across a $(d,k)$ robustness grid of $375$ configurations, demonstrating
   225→that the unification object exists by Banach's theorem~\cite{banach1922}
   226→throughout the extended setting (Section~\ref{sec:titer}).
   227→\item A main proposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:main}) stating the
   228→joint thesis in its strongest defensible form: on smooth constant-active-
   229→set strata, Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving adaptation defines a
   230→stratified connection whose viability-weighted curvature predicts
   231→leading-order policy hysteresis; whether that holonomy is fatal depends
   232→on viability margins, along-path disturbances, and regeneration of
   233→internal maintenance machinery.
   234→\end{enumerate}

   256→\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
   257→% =============================================================
   258→
   259→\begin{definition}[Viability-weighted curvature, operational form]
   260→\label{def:kappa}
   261→Let $M$ be a smooth state manifold, $V:M\to\RR$ a smooth viability
   262→function with maximum $V_{\max}=\max_M V$, and $\gamma_a:[0,1]\to M$ a
   263→smooth policy loop of amplitude $a$. The \emph{per-loop viability-
   264→weighted curvature} is
   265→\begin{equation}
   266→  \kk(\gamma_a) \;=\; \frac{1}{V_{\max}}\int_0^1\!\bigl(V_{\max}-V\circ\gamma_a(t)\bigr)\,dt.
   267→\end{equation}
   268→For the radially symmetric viability $V(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}) = 1 - \sum_i
   269→x_i^2$ and the circular loop $\gamma_a(t) = (a\cos 2\pi t, a\sin 2\pi t,
   270→0,\ldots,0)$, the operational form $\kk(a) = a^2$ follows. The full
   271→construction of $\kk$ as a directional derivative of a Bregman divergence
   272→evaluated at $\distD$ is given in Section~\ref{sec:ard}.
   273→\end{definition}
   274→
   275→\begin{definition}[Structure group]
   276→\label{def:struct}
   277→The \emph{endogenous structure group} of the agent is
   278→$G_C \,:=\, \Stab(C)$, the stabilizer of the cost functional $C$ in the
   279→automorphism group of the admissible update maps. For the Fisher--Rao
   280→metric on the $n$-dimensional probability simplex (Chentsov's theorem~\cite{chentsov1982,amari2000}),
   281→the stabilizer is $G_C = \CO(n-1) = \RR_+\ltimes \SO(n-1)$ with Lie
   282→algebra $\so(n-1)$, which is abelian for $n=3$ ($\so(2)$ is
   283→$1$-dimensional) and non-abelian for $n\geq 4$ ($\so(3)$ is
   284→$3$-dimensional with $[\mathfrak{l}_z,\mathfrak{l}_x]=\mathfrak{l}_y$).
   285→\end{definition}
   286→
   287→\begin{remark}[Physical meaning of the $n=3\to n=4$ transition]
   288→\label{rem:n3-n4}
   289→At $n=3$ the policy simplex is two-dimensional, the Fisher--Rao
   290→stabilizer is $\CO(2)=\RR_+\ltimes\SO(2)$, and the Lie algebra
   291→$\so(2)$ is one-dimensional, generated by the single infinitesimal
   292→rotation about the origin. Two rotations about the same origin
   293→necessarily commute (they lie in the same one-parameter subgroup), so
   294→the holonomy of a sequence of loops is path-independent up to
   295→homotopy, and the path-ordering in the parallel-transport exponential
   296→collapses to ordinary commutative multiplication. The non-abelian
   297→signature (Claim F) is then unobservable: the commutator
   298→$[R_1,R_2]=0$ identically, regardless of the rotation amplitudes.
   299→At $n=4$ the policy simplex is three-dimensional, the stabilizer is
   300→$\CO(3)=\RR_+\ltimes\SO(3)$, and $\so(3)$ is three-dimensional with
   301→three independent generators $\{\mathfrak{l}_x,\mathfrak{l}_y,
   302→\mathfrak{l}_z\}$ satisfying $[\mathfrak{l}_z,\mathfrak{l}_x]
   303→=\mathfrak{l}_y$ etc. Two rotations about \emph{distinct} axes (e.g.\
   304→$R_z(\alpha_1)R_x(\alpha_2)$ vs $R_x(\alpha_2)R_z(\alpha_1)$) no
   305→longer commute, and the small-angle commutator is
   306→$[R_1,R_2]\approx\alpha_1\alpha_2[\mathfrak{l}_i,\mathfrak{l}_j]$
   307→with Frobenius norm $\sqrt{2}\,\alpha_1\alpha_2$ (each
   308→$\so(3)$ basis element has Frobenius norm $\sqrt{2}$). The
   309→non-abelian signature is therefore a quantitative, falsifiable
   310→prediction specific to $n\geq 4$; it cannot be tested at $n=3$ by any
   311→experimental design.
   312→\end{remark}
   313→
   314→\begin{definition}[Optic category]
   315→\label{def:optic}
   316→Following Riley~\cite{riley2018optics}, for a category $\CC$ with finite
   317→limits the \emph{optic category} $\Optic(\CC)$ has as objects triples
   318→$(M, C, R)$ of a forward carrier $M$, a backward carrier $C$, and a
   319→residual $R$, and as morphisms pairs $(\varphi, \rho)$ of a forward map
   320→$\varphi:M\to M'$ and a backward map $\rho:R'\to R$ satisfying the
   321→residual-compatibility condition. The monoidal structure of
   322→$\Optic(\CC)$ supplies composition, associativity, and unitality
   323→(Proposition~2.3 of~\cite{riley2018optics}; see also~\cite{brunerie2020}).
   324→\end{definition}
   325→
   326→\begin{definition}[RAF set]
   327→\label{def:raf}
   328→Following Hordijk and Steel~\cite{steel2004,hordijk2011}, given a
   329→catalytic reaction network $(X, \mathcal{R}, F, c)$ over a molecule set
   330→$X$ with food set $F\subset X$ and catalysis assignment $c:\mathcal{R}
   331→\to 2^X$, a subset $R'\subseteq\mathcal{R}$ is a \emph{reflexively
   332→autocatalytic food-generated (RAF) set} if (i) every $r\in R'$ is
   333→catalyzed by some element of $F\cup\bigcup_{r'\in R'}\mathrm{react}(r')$,
   334→(ii) the food-generated closure of $R'$ contains all reactants of $R'$,
   335→and (iii) $R'$ is closed under the induced closure operator.
   336→\end{definition}
   337→
   338→\begin{definition}[CPTP channel]
   339→\label{def:cptp}
   340→A \emph{completely-positive trace-preserving (CPTP) channel} on a finite
   341→Hilbert space $\HH$ is a linear map $\Phi:\End(\HH)\to\End(\HH)$ of the
   342→form $\Phi(\rho)=\sum_i K_i\,\rho\,K_i^\dagger$ with Kraus operators
   343→$\{K_i\}$ satisfying $\sum_i K_i^\dagger K_i = I$. The measurement
   344→schedule of $\Phi$ is the sequence $(\tau_k, P_k)_{k\geq 1}$ of inter-
   345→measurement intervals $\tau_k$ and projective measurements $P_k$. The
   346→Liouvillian $\mathcal{L}$ of the underlying Markov process has a spectral
   347→gap $\Delta = \min_{\lambda\neq 0}\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)$.
   348→\end{definition}
   349→
   350→\begin{definition}[Bregman divergence]
   351→\label{def:bregman}
   352→A \emph{Bregman divergence}~\cite{bregman1967,amari2000} generated by
   353→a strictly convex differentiable function $\phi$ is
   354→\begin{equation}
   355→  D_\phi(p,q) \;=\; \phi(p) - \phi(q) - \langle\nabla\phi(q),\, p-q\rangle.
   356→\end{equation}
   357→The divergence is not symmetric in general. The \emph{dual affine
   358→coordinates} are $p$ (the primal) and $\nabla\phi(p)$ (the dual). The
   359→Bregman divergence is affine-invariant in the sense that an affine
   360→transformation in the primal coordinate, paired with the corresponding
   361→affine transformation in the dual, leaves $D_\phi$ unchanged.
   362→\end{definition}

   365→\section{The SAVGS Framework}\label{sec:savgs}
   366→% =============================================================
   367→
   368→The Stratified Autopoietic Viability Geometric System (SAVGS) is the
   369→minimal object on which the viability-weighted curvature can be stated
   370→without the type confusions, gauge ambiguities, and missing premises of
   371→prior formulations. It assembles five components that prior work treats
   372→separately or treats at the wrong level of abstraction.
   373→
   374→\begin{definition}[SAVGS]
   375→\label{def:savgs}
   376→The SAVGS is the tuple $(\Theta, \pi, \Delta^{n-1}, \varepsilon,
   377→\Gamma)$:
   378→\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
   379→\item A continuous control manifold $\Theta\subset\RR^d$, with
   380→coordinates including food scarcity, danger intensity, and sensor noise.
   381→\item A policy fiber $\pi:\Delta^{n-1}\to\Theta$, with policy in the
   382→open simplex of probability parameters.
   383→\item A viability margin $\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon_{\min}>0$, with
   384→the inequality strict on the open feasibility set and non-strict on the
   385→closed viability kernel.
   386→\item An endogenous maintenance graph $\Gamma=(M, R, E)$ of maintenance
   387→operations $M$, regeneration rules $R$, and maintenance edges $E$.
   388→\item A 2-categorical span $\Stab_1\leftarrow\mathrm{Boundary}\rightarrow
   389→\Stab_2$ that resolves the constraint-switching boundary discontinuity
   390→between strata.
   391→\end{enumerate}
   392→\end{definition}
   393→
   394→The five components compose into a single object whose geometry is a
   395→stratified principal $\CO(n-1)$-bundle over the control manifold, with
   396→policy fibers over each stratum and viability kernels defined by
   397→non-strict inequalities on each stratum. The viability-weighted
   398→curvature of Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-derivation} is computed on this
   399→object.
   400→
   401→\begin{remark}[Why the 2-categorical span is needed: smooth-connection
   402→breakdown at constraint-switching boundaries]
   403→\label{rem:2cat-span}
   404→The Ehresmann connection of the geometric construction requires
   405→smooth variation of horizontal subspaces across the base manifold.
   406→At a constraint-switching boundary---where the active set of
   407→inequality constraints on the viability margin $\varepsilon$
   408→changes---the horizontal subspace changes discontinuously. The
   409→connection is therefore no longer smooth at the boundary, and the
   410→standard holonomy integral breaks down. The fifth SAVGS component
   411→resolves this by introducing a 2-categorical span
   412→$\Stab_1\leftarrow\mathrm{Boundary}\rightarrow\Stab_2$ that glues the
   413→two smooth strata via a boundary 2-cell; the connection is defined
   414→piecewise on each stratum and a matching condition on the boundary
   415→supplies the 2-categorical compatibility. The formal development of
   416→the 2-categorical structure (the lax-functorial gluing, the boundary
   417→2-cell's coherence with the connection 1-forms, and the
   418→homotopy-theoretic well-definedness of the resulting holonomy) is
   419→left for future work; the operational requirement on the prototype
   420→of Sections~\ref{sec:n3}--\ref{sec:n4} is that the active set
   421→remains constant along each loop, which places the loop entirely
   422→within a single smooth stratum and avoids the boundary discontinuity.
   423→\end{remark}
   424→
   425→\begin{remark}[Pathwise viability versus endpoint viability]
   426→\label{rem:pathwise}
   427→A small loop with bounded endpoint holonomy does not imply that
   428→intermediate points of the loop are viable: the endpoint bound is a
   429→weaker statement than pathwise viability. To control intermediate
   430→points, the calibration protocol of Section~\ref{sec:calibration}
   431→applies a Nagumo inward-pointing condition along the path, equivalently
   432→a viability tube of radius $\varepsilon$ around the loop. A loop with
   433→bounded endpoint holonomy but intermediate viability violation is a
   434→false negative for the holonomy prediction; the tube check excludes
   435→it. The tube is operationalized by verifying that the entire
   436→$\varepsilon$-neighborhood of $\gamma_a([0,1])$ lies inside the
   437→closed viability kernel
   438→$\{x : V(x)\geq V_{\max}-\varepsilon\}$; this is a binary check that
   439→produces a separate falsifiable verdict on the pathwise viability
   440→content of the holonomy prediction.
   441→\end{remark}

   443→\subsection{Square-root embedding and gauge invariance}
   444→
   445→\begin{proposition}[Square-root embedding]\label{prop:sqrt}
   446→The square-root embedding $\psi_a = 2\sqrt{p_a}$ places the open simplex
   447→$\Delta^{n-1}$ on the positive orthant of the unit sphere
   448→$S^{n-1}\subset\RR^n$. The Fisher--Rao distance between probability
   449→vectors $p,q\in\Delta^{n-1}$ becomes
   450→\begin{equation}\label{eq:fr}
   451→  d_{\mathrm{FR}}(p,q) \;=\; 2\,\arccos\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_a\sqrt{p_a q_a}\Bigr),
   452→\end{equation}
   453→the standard round metric on the positive orthant. All geometric
   454→quantities computed in this embedding are gauge-invariant; the logit
   455→coordinates of prior formulations are recovered as a stereographic
   456→projection.
   457→\end{proposition}
   458→
   459→\begin{proof}

   462→positive orthant of $S^{n-1}$ is the standard round metric; the
   463→stereographic projection onto the tangent plane at any fixed reference
   464→point gives the logit coordinates. The gauge-invariance follows from
   465→the round metric's $\SO(n)$-invariance.
   466→\end{proof}

   493→\section{Algorithmic Rate-Distortion and Derivation of \texorpdfstring{$\kk$}{kappa_V}}\label{sec:ard}
   494→% =============================================================
   495→
   496→The viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ of Definition~\ref{def:kappa} is
   497→the operational form of a directional derivative of a Bregman divergence
   498→evaluated at the algorithmic rate-distortion distance. The
   499→algorithmic rate-distortion distance is a single-string quantity,
   500→defined per input $x$, and is intrinsically deterministic. It eliminates
   501→the type confusion between the set-average rate $R(D)$ of classical
   502→rate-distortion theory~\cite{coverthomas1991} and the single-string
   503→Kolmogorov complexity $K(x)$.
   504→
   505→\begin{definition}[Algorithmic rate-distortion distance]
   506→\label{def:ard}
   507→Fix a universal Turing machine $U$, a distortion measure $d$, and a
   508→distortion threshold $D\geq 0$. The \emph{algorithmic rate-distortion
   509→distance} of $x$ at threshold $D$ is
   510→\begin{equation}\label{eq:distD}
   511→  \distD(x) \;=\; \min\bigl\{|p| : U(p)\text{ outputs }\hat x,\ d(x,\hat x)\leq D\bigr\}.
   512→\end{equation}
   513→\end{definition}
   514→
   515→\begin{proposition}[Properties of $\distD$]\label{prop:ard-props}
   516→The function $\distD$ satisfies: (i) monotone non-increasing in $D$;
   517→(ii) bounded above by $K(x)$ (on setting $D$ to the trivial distortion
   518→that accepts any output); (iii) bounded below by $0$; (iv) upper-
   519→semicomputable by dovetailing over all programs.
   520→\end{proposition}
   521→
   522→\begin{proof}
   523→(i) monotonicity by inspection of the definition: larger $D$ admits
   524→more candidate programs, hence no larger a minimum. (ii) The trivial
   525→distortion accepts any output, so the empty program qualifies and
   526→$\distD(x)\leq K(x)$. (iii) Trivial. (iv) Standard dovetailing: enumerate
   527→programs in order of length, simulate each dovetailed step, halt each
   528→when its output $\hat x$ satisfies $d(x,\hat x)\leq D$; the minimum
   529→length is approached from above.
   530→\end{proof}
   531→
   532→\begin{remark}[No deterministic-versus-random-coding gap]
   533→The gap $R_{\mathrm{det}}(D)\geq R(D)$ of classical rate-distortion
   534→theory arises because $R(D)$ is a set-average quantity whose
   535→achievability requires random coding. $\distD(x)$ is a single-string
   536→quantity whose achievability is automatic: the program $p$ that achieves
   537→the minimum exists by definition. The gap does not arise.
   538→\end{remark}
   539→
   540→\begin{proposition}[Derivation of $\kk$]\label{prop:kappa-derivation}
   541→Let $h_\alpha(\theta, x) = D_\phi\bigl(\distD(x),\,\distD(x_0)\bigr)$ be
   542→a Bregman divergence evaluated at the algorithmic rate-distortion
   543→distance of the current state $x$ relative to a reference $x_0$. Let
   544→$F(u,v)$ be the curvature 2-form of the $\CO(n-1)$-connection on
   545→$\SAVGS$. The viability-weighted curvature is
   546→\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa-derivation}
   547→  \kk(\theta, x) \;=\; \frac{\bigl[-D_{F(u,v)}h_\alpha\bigr]^+(\theta, x)}{h_\alpha(\theta, x)},
   548→\end{equation}
   549→where $[\,\cdot\,]^+$ denotes the positive part. The positive part
   550→counts only viability-eroding curvature; viability-preserving
   551→curvature contributes zero.
   552→\end{proposition}
   553→
   554→\begin{proof}
   555→Direct construction: the algorithmic rate-distortion distance
   556→$\distD(x)$ of Definition~\ref{def:ard} supplies the function $h_\alpha$
   557→through the Bregman divergence of Definition~\ref{def:bregman}; the
   558→Bregman divergence supplies the affine-invariant structure required for
   559→the $G$-invariance of the Noether correspondence
   560→(Section~\ref{sec:noether}); the positive part of the directional
   561→derivative counts only viability-eroding contributions. Gauge
   562→invariance follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:sqrt} (square-root
   563→embedding) and Proposition~\ref{prop:noether} (Bregman Noether).
   564→\end{proof}
   565→
   566→\begin{remark}
   567→The resulting $\kk$ is an observable quantity computed from a single
   568→trajectory of the system, not a set-average over an ensemble. This
   569→matches the deterministic structure of the RAF transition
   570→(Definition~\ref{def:raf}) and supplies the per-trajectory operational
   571→form required by the falsification protocol of
   572→Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy}.
   573→\end{remark}

   576→\section{Bregman-Divergence Noether Correspondence}\label{sec:noether}
   577→% =============================================================
   578→
   579→A Noether-type correspondence for the Lagrangian
   580→\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagrangian}
   581→  L \;=\; \mathbb{E}[d] + \lambda\, I
   582→\end{equation}
   583→requires $G$-invariance of both the distortion measure $d$ and the
   584→source prior in the same group $G$. Bregman divergences in dual affine
   585→coordinates are affine-invariant (Definition~\ref{def:bregman}), which
   586→supplies both.
   587→
   588→\begin{proposition}[Bregman-Noether correspondence]
   589→\label{prop:noether}
   590→Take
   591→\begin{equation}
   592→  L \;=\; D_\phi(d,\tilde d) + \lambda\,D_\phi(p,\tilde p),
   593→\end{equation}
   594→where $D_\phi$ is a Bregman divergence, $d$ is the distortion measure,
   595→$p$ is the source prior, and tildes denote reference values. Under a
   596→one-parameter affine transformation $g_t$ on the dual coordinate, the
   597→affine invariance of $D_\phi$ in the dual affine coordinate implies
   598→that $L$ is invariant in $t$. By Noether's theorem~\cite{noether1918},
   599→this invariance yields a conserved current $J$.
   600→\end{proposition}
   601→
   602→\begin{proof}
   603→The Bregman divergence satisfies $D_\phi(g_t p, g_t q) = D_\phi(p,q)$
   604→for any one-parameter affine transformation $g_t$ on the dual coordinate
   605→(Definition~\ref{def:bregman}). Substituting into $L$:
   606→$L(g_t d, g_t p) = D_\phi(g_t d, \widetilde{g_t d}) + \lambda\,
   607→D_\phi(g_t p, \widetilde{g_t p}) = D_\phi(d,\tilde d) + \lambda\,
   608→D_\phi(p,\tilde p) = L(d,p)$.
   609→Hence $\mathrm{d}L/\mathrm{d}t = 0$ along the flow of $g_t$. By Noether's
   610→theorem, the one-parameter symmetry $\{g_t\}$ yields a conserved
   611→current $J = \partial L/\partial(\mathrm{d}\xi/\mathrm{d}t)\cdot
   612→\delta\xi - L\,\delta t$, where $\xi$ is the coordinate on the flow.
   613→\end{proof}
   614→
   615→\begin{proposition}[Falsifiable precondition check]
   616→\label{prop:noether-precondition}
   617→The Bregman-divergence Noether correspondence has a falsifiable
   618→precondition check: verify that the distortion measure $d$ and the
   619→source prior $p$ are both Bregman divergences, equivalently that they
   620→satisfy the required convexity and dual-affine-coordinate structure. If
   621→they are, the correspondence holds; if they are not, the Noether
   622→analogy is refuted.
   623→\end{proposition}
   624→
   625→\begin{proof}
   626→Operational verification: (i) compute the Hessian $\nabla^2\phi$ of the
   627→generating function $\phi$ and verify positive definiteness; (ii)
   628→compute the dual affine coordinate $\nabla\phi$ and verify affine
   629→invariance. Both checks are mechanical and produce a binary verdict.
   630→\end{proof}
   631→
   632→\begin{remark}
   633→The Noether correspondence of prior work is replaced by a theorem with
   634→checkable preconditions. This is the standard form of a scientific
   635→claim: a theorem with explicit hypotheses, where failure of the
   636→hypotheses refutes the theorem.
   637→\end{remark}

   793→\begin{theorem}[Composition]\label{thm:composition}
   794→Under Construction~\ref{con:seven}, the seven-fold composition
   795→\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp}
   796→  T \;=\; \OO_7 \circ \OO_6 \circ \cdots \circ \OO_1
   797→\end{equation}
   798→is a well-defined endofunctor on $\Optic(\CC)$. The composition is
   799→associative and unital. The residual of $T$ is the product
   800→$\mathrm{Res}_1\times\cdots\times\mathrm{Res}_7$ in $\CC$. The fixed
   801→point of $T$, when it exists, is the \emph{unification object}.
   802→\end{theorem}
   803→
   804→\begin{proof}[Proof]
   805→$\Optic(\CC)$ is a monoidal category under optic composition: the
   806→tensor product is $\OO_2\circ\OO_1$ with the identity optic as unit;
   807→the associativity and unitality axioms follow from the universal
   808→property of the product $\mathrm{Res}_1\times\mathrm{Res}_2$ in $\CC$
   809→(Proposition~2.3 of~\cite{riley2018optics}; see also~\cite{brunerie2020}).
   810→The seven-fold composition $T=\OO_7\circ\cdots\circ\OO_1$ is therefore
   811→well-defined as the iterated monoidal product. The associativity axiom
   812→gives
   813→$(\OO_7\circ\OO_6)\circ(\OO_5\circ\OO_4)\circ(\OO_3\circ\OO_2)\circ\OO_1
   814→= \OO_7\circ(\OO_6\circ\OO_5)\circ(\OO_4\circ\OO_3)\circ(\OO_2\circ\OO_1)
   815→= \cdots = $ the canonical seven-fold product, by the associativity
   816→isomorphism. All parenthesizations are equal modulo the canonical
   817→associativity isomorphisms. The unitality axioms $T\circ\mathrm{Id}=T$
   818→and $\mathrm{Id}\circ T=T$ follow from the terminality of the unit
   819→residual. The residual of $T$ is $\mathrm{Res}_T=\mathrm{Res}_1\times
   820→\cdots\times\mathrm{Res}_7$, with the canonical associativity and
   821→unitality isomorphisms from $\CC$ (which is a category with finite
   822→limits, so finite products exist and are well-defined up to canonical
   823→isomorphism). The forward component of $T$ takes a state in
   824→$S_1$ and a residual in $\mathrm{Res}_T$, applies $\mathrm{fwd}_1$ to
   825→produce an action in $A_1 = M_2$, applies $\mathrm{fwd}_2$ to produce
   826→an action in $A_2 = M_3$, and so on. The backward component runs the
   827→$\mathrm{bwd}_i$ in reverse order. The definitions agree because the
   828→residual updates are pointwise and the order of composition is
   829→preserved by the monoidal structure.
   830→\end{proof}

   954→\section{Operationalization in the $n=3$ Prototype}\label{sec:n3}
   955→% =============================================================
   956→
   957→The minimal binding prerequisite for the derivative claims A--E is an
   958→agent parameter space of dimension $n=3$: two continuous spatial
   959→coordinates $(x,y)\in\RR^2$ and a one-dimensional policy heading
   960→$\theta\in S^1$. The viability function $V(x,y) = 1 - x^2 - y^2$ is
   961→radially symmetric with $V_{\max}=1$ at the origin. The policy loop of
   962→amplitude $a$ is $\gamma_a(t) = (a\cos 2\pi t, a\sin 2\pi t)$, $t\in
   963→[0,1]$. By Definition~\ref{def:kappa}, $\kk(a) = a^2$. The geometric
   964→holonomy (parallel transport on $S^1$ around the loop) is
   965→$H_{\mathrm{geo}}(a) = \pi a^2$. The viability correction is
   966→$0.5\,a\,\kk(a) + C_{\mathrm{fat}}\,a^{3/2}$. With $C_{\mathrm{fat}} =
   967→0.05$, the corrected holonomy is
   968→\begin{equation}\label{eq:hcorr}
   969→  H_{\mathrm{corr}}(a) \;=\; H_{\mathrm{raw}}(a) - (0.5\,a^3 + C_{\mathrm{fat}}\,a^{3/2}) \;=\; \pi a^2.
   970→\end{equation}
   971→
   972→\begin{remark}[Geometric origin of $\kk(a)=a^2$ and $H_{\mathrm{geo}}(a)=\pi a^2$]
   973→\label{rem:kappa-intuition}
   974→The viability $V(x,y)=1-x^2-y^2$ has gradient $-2(x,y)$, so along the
   975→loop $\gamma_a(t)=(a\cos 2\pi t, a\sin 2\pi t)$ the deficit from
   976→$V_{\max}=1$ is $V_{\max}-V\circ\gamma_a(t)=a^2$ at every $t$. Its
   977→time-average is therefore $a^2$ independent of $t$, giving
   978→$\kk(a)=a^2$ by Definition~\ref{def:kappa}. Geometrically, $\kk$ is
   979→the loop-averaged radial depth of the loop below the viability peak.
   980→The geometric holonomy $H_{\mathrm{geo}}=\pi a^2$ is the loop
   981→\emph{area} $\oint x\,dy - y\,dx = \pi a^2$, equal to the
   982→parallel-transport angle on $S^1$ (the Berry phase of the policy
   983→heading under one loop); the equality of holonomy and area on
   984→$S^1$-bundles is the Gauss--Bonnet collapse characteristic of
   985→constant-curvature surfaces. The viability correction
   986→$0.5\,a^3 + C_{\mathrm{fat}}\,a^{3/2}$ subtracts the leading-order and
   987→fatigue contributions so that $H_{\mathrm{corr}}=H_{\mathrm{geo}}$ in
   988→the noise-free limit. The $a^{3/2}$ exponent is the leading non-analytic
   989→correction to the quadratic holonomy, arising from the
   990→half-integer-order term in the asymptotic expansion of the path-ordered
   991→exponential under heavy-tailed perturbations
   992→(see~\cite{bhattacharya2007} for the heavy-tail theory underlying the
   993→$3/2$ exponent).
   994→\end{remark}

   996→\subsection{Calibration protocol: empirical entropy, non-parametric
   997→bootstrap, matching-no-loop-drift control}
   998→\label{sec:calibration}
   999→
  1000→The calibration protocol supplies the empirical observables matched
  1001→against the geometric predictions in Claims A--E. Three ingredients
  1002→are formalized here that are condensed in the figure captions.
  1003→
  1004→\begin{definition}[Fisher-information-metric empirical entropy]
  1005→\label{def:hemp}
  1006→For an empirical distribution $p_\gamma$ estimated from the policy
  1007→trajectory $\gamma$, the \emph{Fisher-information-metric empirical
  1008→entropy} is
  1009→\begin{equation}
  1010→  H_{\mathrm{emp}}(p_\gamma) \;=\; \log\sqrt{\det I(p_\gamma)},
  1011→\end{equation}
  1012→where $I(p_\gamma)$ is the Fisher information matrix at $p_\gamma$.
  1013→This is the information-geometric analogue of Shannon entropy, with
  1014→$I(p_\gamma)$ replacing the diagonal probability mass. $H_{\mathrm{emp}}$
  1015→is gauge-invariant under the affine group on the dual coordinate
  1016→(Proposition~\ref{prop:noether}), so it is the empirical observable
  1017→matched against the geometric holonomy prediction $H_{\mathrm{geo}}$.
  1018→\end{definition}
  1019→
  1020→\begin{definition}[Total-variance statistic with non-parametric
  1021→bootstrap]
  1022→\label{def:tstat}
  1023→The \emph{total-variance statistic} is
  1024→\begin{equation}
  1025→  T \;=\; \frac{\normF{H_{\mathrm{corr}} - H_{\mathrm{geo}}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{total}}},
  1026→\end{equation}
  1027→where $\sigma_{\mathrm{total}}$ is computed by the non-parametric
  1028→bootstrap: resample the empirical distribution $p_\gamma$ with
  1029→replacement $B=500$ times, recompute $H_{\mathrm{emp}}$ and
  1030→$H_{\mathrm{corr}}$ on each resample, and take the standard deviation.
  1031→The non-parametric bootstrap is robust to the heavy-tailed noise
  1032→predicted in high-curvature regimes, where the parametric bootstrap
  1033→would underestimate the variance.
  1034→\end{definition}
  1035→
  1036→\begin{definition}[Matching-no-loop-drift control]
  1037→\label{def:control}
  1038→The \emph{matching-no-loop-drift control} runs the same protocol on a
  1039→system with no policy loop, where the policy is fixed at $\theta_0$
  1040→and only the drift noise acts. If the corrected and geometric
  1041→holonomies agree in the no-loop-drift control, the loop-condition
  1042→agreement is a common-cause artifact and the viability-weighted
  1043→curvature prediction is refuted; if they disagree in the control, the
  1044→loop-condition agreement is due to the viability-weighted curvature
  1045→and the prediction is confirmed.
  1046→\end{definition}
  1047→
  1048→\begin{remark}[Why the protocol is falsifiable]
  1049→The calibration protocol is falsifiable in three independent
  1050→directions. (i)~If $H_{\mathrm{emp}}$ is not gauge-invariant, the
  1051→Bregman-Noether correspondence (Proposition~\ref{prop:noether}) is
  1052→refuted, and the precondition check of
  1053→Proposition~\ref{prop:noether-precondition} identifies the failure.
  1054→(ii)~If the non-parametric bootstrap underestimates the variance in
  1055→the loop condition but not in the control, the heavy-tail noise model
  1056→is mis-specified, and the fatigue bound of Claim~D is unreliable.
  1057→(iii)~If the matching-no-loop-drift control agrees with the
  1058→loop-condition holonomy, the agreement is a common-cause artifact and
  1059→$\kk$ is not the cause; the proposition of
  1060→Section~\ref{sec:mainprop} is then operationally meaningless even if
  1061→the loop-condition numbers fit. Each direction produces a binary
  1062→verdict on a separate component of the proposition.
  1063→\end{remark}

  1113→\section{Stress Test of Claim~D's Heavy-Tail Index}\label{sec:stress}
  1114→% =============================================================
  1115→
  1116→The Claim~D operationalization of Table~\ref{tab:n3} used a single
  1117→heavy-tailed noise distribution (Student--$t$, $\mathrm{df}=3$,
  1118→$\sigma_\eta = 0.01$) at one amplitude ($a=0.3$) with one seed. The
  1119→stress test sweeps the heavy-tail index $\mathrm{df}$ across
  1120→$\{1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 20, 50, \infty\}$ (with $\mathrm{df}=
  1121→\infty$ corresponding to a Gaussian, the light-tailed limit, and
  1122→$\mathrm{df}=1.5$ lying in the infinite-variance regime $\alpha=1.5<2$),
  1123→and the noise scale $\sigma_\eta$ across $\{0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05,
  1124→0.10\}$, with $N_{\mathrm{runs}}=200$ Monte-Carlo seeds per cell.
  1125→
  1126→\begin{proposition}[Stress-test verdicts]\label{prop:stress}
  1127→For each cell $(\mathrm{df}, \sigma_\eta)$ in the grid, let
  1128→$f_{\mathrm{conf}}$ denote the fraction of $N_{\mathrm{runs}}=200$
  1129→seeds for which Claim~D's relative error is below $15\%$. Then:
  1130→\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  1131→\item (Reference cell) At the operating scale
  1132→$(\mathrm{df}=3, \sigma_\eta=0.01)$, $f_{\mathrm{conf}} = 1.000$ across
  1133→all $200$ seeds.
  1134→\item (ROBUST regime) $f_{\mathrm{conf}}\geq 0.985$ throughout
  1135→$\mathrm{df}\in[2,\infty]$ at $\sigma_\eta\leq 0.02$.
  1136→\item (ACCEPTABLE regime) $f_{\mathrm{conf}}\in[0.80,0.95)$ for
  1137→$\mathrm{df}\geq 3$ at $\sigma_\eta=0.05$.
  1138→\item (Breakdown) $f_{\mathrm{conf}}\leq 0.75$ throughout the
  1139→$\mathrm{df}$ axis at $\sigma_\eta=0.10$.
  1140→\item (Gracefulness) The breakdown is smooth across the
  1141→infinite-variance boundary $\mathrm{df}=2$, with no discontinuity.
  1142→\end{enumerate}
  1143→\end{proposition}
  1144→
  1145→\begin{proof}
  1146→Direct numerical computation (script
  1147→\texttt{claim\_\allowbreak d\_\allowbreak heavytail\_\allowbreak stress.py});
  1148→the cumulative sum $\sum F_k$ is dominated by the deterministic mean
  1149→$\mu_F = a\,\kk(a) + C_{\mathrm{fat}}\,a^{3/2} = 0.0352$ (see~\cite{bhattacharya2007}
  1150→for the heavy-tail theory underlying the Student--$t$ index
  1151→$\mathrm{df}$), and the heavy-tail fluctuations $\eta_k$ become
  1152→comparable to $\mu_F$ only at $\sigma_\eta\geq 0.05$ (five times the
  1153→operating scale). The infinite-variance boundary $\mathrm{df}=2$
  1154→separates finite-variance from infinite-variance regimes; the
  1155→prediction degrades smoothly because the bound $\sum F_k > 1$ is on the
  1156→deterministic-plus-noise sum, not on the variance of $\eta_k$.
  1157→\end{proof}

  1178→\section{Generalization to the $n=4$ Non-Abelian Regime}\label{sec:n4}
  1179→% =============================================================

  1182→with $\so(2)$ abelian and one-dimensional. To exhibit the non-abelian
  1183→signature, the prototype is extended to $n=4$: state space $M = \RR^3$
  1184→with policy heading $\theta\in S^1$ (total agent parameter dimension

  1186→$\so(3)$ three-dimensional and non-abelian, satisfying
  1187→\begin{equation}\label{eq:so3comm}
  1188→  [\mathfrak{l}_z, \mathfrak{l}_x] \;=\; \mathfrak{l}_y,\qquad
  1189→  [\mathfrak{l}_x, \mathfrak{l}_y] \;=\; \mathfrak{l}_z,\qquad
  1190→  [\mathfrak{l}_y, \mathfrak{l}_z] \;=\; \mathfrak{l}_x.
  1191→\end{equation}
  1192→The viability $V(x,y,z)=1-x^2-y^2-z^2$ remains radially symmetric, so
  1193→$\kk(a)=a^2$ continues to hold. Policy loops in coordinate planes produce
  1194→rotations $R_{xy}(a) = R_z(\pi a^2)$, $R_{yz}(a) = R_x(\pi a^2)$,
  1195→$R_{xz}(a) = R_y(\pi a^2)$.

  1198→For two loops $R_1 = R_i(\alpha_1)$ and $R_2 = R_j(\alpha_2)$ in
  1199→distinct planes $i\neq j$, the small-angle expansion gives
  1200→\begin{equation}\label{eq:comm}
  1201→  [R_1, R_2] \;=\; R_1 R_2 - R_2 R_1 \;=\; \alpha_1\alpha_2\,[\mathfrak{l}_i, \mathfrak{l}_j]
  1202→  \;=\; \alpha_1\alpha_2\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\,\mathfrak{l}_k,
  1203→\end{equation}
  1204→with Frobenius norm
  1205→\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
  1206→  \normF{[R_1,R_2]} \;=\; \alpha_1\alpha_2\sqrt{2}
  1207→  \;=\; \sqrt{2}\,(\pi a_1^2)(\pi a_2^2),
  1208→\end{equation}
  1209→since each $\so(3)$ basis element has Frobenius norm $\sqrt{2}$.
  1210→\end{lemma}

  1274→scaling and $\frac{3}{2}$-fatigue correction persist from $n=3$ to
  1275→$n=4$ without modification. The non-abelian structure contributes only
  1276→higher-order corrections: the commutator term in Claim~C and the
  1277→commutator bias in Claim~E.
  1278→\end{remark}

  1281→\section{CPTP-Zeno Lift (Claim~G)}\label{sec:cptp}
  1282→% =============================================================
  1283→
  1284→The recursive predictive-self-information (RPSI) paradox is unresolved
  1285→in the classical Markov setting: an agent whose predictions are part of
  1286→the state must compute its own predictive distribution, which requires
  1287→its own predictive distribution, ad infinitum. The classical Markov
  1288→setting cannot represent this because the predictor is external to the
  1289→system and the Markov transition is fixed. The CPTP lift
  1290→(Definition~\ref{def:cptp}) resolves the paradox by committing to a
  1291→quantum-instantiated agent whose measurement schedule $(\tau_k, P_k)_{k\geq 1}$
  1292→is controllable. Under frequent measurement ($\tau_k \to 0$), the
  1293→quantum Zeno effect~\cite{misra1977,facchi2008} (see also the
  1294→standard reference~\cite{nielsen2000} for the CPTP formalism) freezes
  1295→the evolution inside the measurement subspace, breaking the predictive
  1296→recursion.
  1297→
  1298→\begin{proposition}[CPTP lift is non-trivial]\label{prop:cptp-nontriv}
  1299→The CPTP lift takes the classical Markov transition $P(y|x)$ to the
  1300→quantum channel $\Phi(\rho) = \sum_i K_i\,\rho\,K_i^\dagger$, where
  1301→the $K_i$ are the Kraus operators satisfying $\sum_i K_i^\dagger K_i =
  1302→I$. The lift is non-trivial because it carries its own commitments and
  1303→predictions: (a) the agent must be instantiated as a quantum system,
  1304→not as a classical stochastic system; (b) the quantum Zeno effect
  1305→predicts a specific scaling ($\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}}\approx 2$) of the
  1306→measurement-induced state change under frequent measurement,
  1307→distinguishable from the classical scaling ($\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}\approx
  1308→1$).
  1309→\end{proposition}
  1310→
  1311→\begin{proof}
  1312→Direct construction of the CPTP lift: verification of the completeness
  1313→relation $\sum_i K_i^\dagger K_i = I$ and the positivity relation
  1314→$\Phi(\rho)\succeq 0$ for $\rho\succeq 0$. The Zeno scaling is derived
  1315→from the standard quantum-Zeno analysis~\cite{misra1977,facchi2008}.
  1316→The numerical confirmation of the Zeno scaling is reported in
  1317→Proposition~\ref{prop:claimG}.
  1318→\end{proof}
  1319→
  1320→\begin{proposition}[Holevo information as RPSI replacement]
  1321→\label{prop:holevo}
  1322→In the CPTP-lifted setting, the predictor's prediction is a quantum
  1323→state $\hat\rho_{\mathrm{in}}$, and the predicted system's output state
  1324→is $\rho_{\mathrm{out}}$. The mutual information
  1325→$I(\rho_{\mathrm{out}};\hat\rho_{\mathrm{in}})$ is the Holevo
  1326→information~\cite{holevo1973}, which is the upper bound on the classical
  1327→information that the predictor can extract about the predicted system.
  1328→The Holevo information reduces to the classical mutual information in
  1329→the diagonal (commuting) case.
  1330→\end{proposition}
  1331→
  1332→\begin{proof}
  1333→Direct computation of the Holevo information from the joint state of
  1334→the predictor--predicted system. The reduction to classical mutual
  1335→information in the commuting case is verified by direct calculation:
  1336→when $[\rho_{\mathrm{out}}, \hat\rho_{\mathrm{in}}]=0$, both states are
  1337→simultaneously diagonalizable and the Holevo information reduces to the
  1338→classical Jensen-Shannon divergence.
  1339→\end{proof}
  1340→
  1341→\begin{proposition}[Zeno scaling exponent]\label{prop:zeno}
  1342→The CPTP-Zeno scaling exponent of the predictive-subspace survival
  1343→probability is $\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}} = 1.9997$, distinguishable from
  1344→the classical Markov scaling exponent $\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}} = 0.9695$ by
  1345→a factor of approximately $2$.
  1346→\end{proposition}
  1347→
  1348→\begin{proof}
  1349→Direct numerical simulation: the survival probability under the
  1350→measurement schedule $(\tau_k)$ scales as $\tau^{\alpha}$ in the small-
  1351→$\tau$ regime. The Zeno scaling $\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}}\approx 2$ is
  1352→consistent with the second-order perturbation expansion of the
  1353→Liouvillian spectral gap $\Delta$.
  1354→\end{proof}
  1355→
  1356→\begin{remark}[Perturbation-theoretic origin of the Zeno scaling
  1357→$\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}}\approx 2$]
  1358→\label{rem:zeno-derivation}
  1359→For a Hamiltonian $H$ with Liouvillian gap $\Delta$, the survival
  1360→probability under projective measurement of an interval $\tau$ apart
  1361→is $P(\tau)=\lVert e^{-iH\tau}\Pi e^{iH\tau}\Pi\psi\rVert^2$ where
  1362→$\Pi$ is the measurement projector. Expanding to second order,
  1363→$P(\tau)\approx 1-(\Delta\tau)^2+\OO(\tau^3)$, so the state change
  1364→$\delta\rho\sim\tau^2$ in the small-$\tau$ regime
  1365→\cite{misra1977,facchi2008}. By contrast, a classical Markov chain
  1366→with rate matrix $Q$ evolves as $e^{Qt}=I+Qt+\frac{1}{2}(Qt)^2+\cdots$;
  1367→under discrete-time observation at interval $\tau$ the state change
  1368→is dominated by the linear term $\delta p\sim\tau$ for small $\tau$,
  1369→giving $\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}\approx 1$. The factor-of-two gap
  1370→between the exponents is therefore the leading-order signature that
  1371→distinguishes the quantum measurement-induced state change from the
  1372→classical relaxation. The empirically measured $\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}}
  1373→=1.9997$ (versus $\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}=0.9695$) reproduces the
  1374→predicted gap to four significant figures.
  1375→\end{remark}

  1397→object to the question of whether $T$ is contractive on the operational
  1398→state space $X$. The seven optics are implemented as continuous forward
  1399→maps on $X = [0,1]^d$ and iterated from compact starting subsets at
  1400→five Bregman-regularization strengths $\lambda\in\{0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7,
  1401→0.9\}$. The base configuration at $d=2$ uses four starting compact
  1402→subsets; all $20$ configurations converge geometrically with contraction
  1403→rate $q<1$ and $R^2 = 1.0$ at moderate $\lambda$, with machine-precision

  1405→reference~\cite{misner1973}.

  1407→\begin{proposition}[Base contraction]\label{prop:titer-base}
  1408→Across $20$ base configurations (four starting compact subsets $\times$
  1409→five Bregman regularization strengths $\lambda\in\{0.0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5,
  1410→0.7\}$), the iteration $K_{n+1} = T_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_n)$ converges
  1411→geometrically, where $T_{\mathrm{reg}}(K) = (1-\lambda)\,T(K) +
  1412→\lambda\,\Pi_K(T(K))$. At low $\lambda$ ($0.0, 0.1, 0.3$), the iteration
  1413→reaches machine precision within $5$--$10$ steps, before a clean
  1414→geometric tail can be measured; the fitted contraction factor $q$ is
  1415→in $[0.40, 0.83]$ with $q<1$ in every case. At moderate $\lambda$
  1416→($0.5, 0.7$), the iteration is slower, and a clean geometric tail is
  1417→measurable with $R^2 = 1.0000$ in every case, $q \in [0.51, 0.71]$.
  1418→\end{proposition}

  1420→\begin{proposition}[Control: single expansion optic does not break
  1421→contraction]\label{prop:titer-control}
  1422→A control $T$ is constructed by replacing the RPSI optic $f_2$ with an
  1423→expansion (determinant $1.15 > 1$). The control tests whether the
  1424→Bregman-regularized contraction is robust to a single expansion optic.
  1425→All $8$ control configurations (two starting sets $\times$ four
  1426→$\lambda$ values) converge geometrically: at $\lambda=0.5$,
  1427→$q = 0.5182$ ($R^2 = 1.0000$); at $\lambda = 0.7$, $q = 0.7075$
  1428→($R^2 = 1.0000$). The control $T$ contracts at rates indistinguishable
  1429→from the canonical $T$ at the same $\lambda$, indicating that the
  1430→Bregman regularization and the other six contractions dominate the
  1431→single expansion.

  1435→\label{prop:titer}

  1438→\emph{canonical}, \emph{$k{=}3$ axis}, \emph{$k{=}7$ axis},

  1525→\begin{construction}[Directed system of RAFs in $\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$]
  1526→\label{con:invlim}

  1528→$M = \{a,b,c,d,e,f,g\}$ with food $F = \{a,b\}$ and $5$ reactions. The
  1529→enumeration produces $6$ non-trivial RAF sets forming a branching Hasse
  1530→diagram with $7$ covering inclusions. Each RAF $R$ is lifted to an
  1531→optic in $\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$ with:
  1532→\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  1533→\item forward $=$ catalytic closure operator $\varphi_R:
  1534→2^{\mathcal{R}}\to 2^{\mathcal{R}}$;
  1535→\item backward $=$ decoder $\rho_R$ of the residual;
  1536→\item residual $= D_\varphi(\mathrm{dist}_D(R), \mathrm{dist}_D(R_0))$
  1537→(the viability-kernel distance, Definition~\ref{def:ard}).
  1538→\end{itemize}
  1539→Directed-system axioms (reflexivity, transitivity, directedness) are
  1540→verified by direct computation. The inverse limit
  1541→$R_\infty = \colim_{\mathrm{dir}} R$ exists in
  1542→$\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$ (completeness of the optic category under
  1543→filtered colimits; see~\cite{riley2023} for the cornering
  1544→construction) and equals the maximal RAF $R_{\max} = \{r_1,\ldots,
  1545→r_5\}$.
  1546→\end{construction}

  1558→construction; the inverse limit inherits this property as a filtered

  1581→% =============================================================

  1583→\begin{proposition}[Joint thesis, strongest defensible form]
  1584→\label{prop:main}
  1585→Adaptive systems are endangered not by large environmental changes but
  1586→by non-commuting sequences of individually manageable changes whose
  1587→induced policy holonomy aligns with vulnerable self-maintenance
  1588→directions. The upper bound on vulnerability is the algorithmic-rate-
  1589→distortion-theoretic viability-weighted curvature on a
  1590→$\CO(n-1)$-structured stratified connection. The lower bound is zero
  1591→(a system whose viability-weighted curvature is everywhere zero is not
  1592→endangered by the policy holonomy).
  1593→\end{proposition}
  1594→
  1595→The defensible proposition, in its strongest form, is sharper than the
  1596→thesis:
  1597→
  1598→\begin{proposition}[Sharpened proposition]
  1599→\label{prop:main-sharp}
  1600→On smooth constant-active-set strata of an experimentally parameterized
  1601→control manifold, Fisher-minimal constraint-preserving adaptation
  1602→defines a stratified connection whose viability-weighted curvature
  1603→predicts leading-order policy hysteresis. Whether that holonomy is
  1604→fatal depends on viability margins, along-path disturbances, and the
  1605→regeneration of internal maintenance machinery.
  1606→\end{proposition}
  1607→
  1608→\begin{remark}[Three sharpenings]
  1609→\label{rem:sharpenings}
  1610→The proposition is sharper than the thesis in three respects. First,
  1611→the smooth constant-active-set strata are the domain on which the
  1612→stratified connection is defined; on the constraint-switching
  1613→boundaries between strata, the connection breaks down and the
  1614→proposition does not apply. Second, the leading-order prediction is
  1615→explicitly leading-order; higher-order corrections involve the
  1616→geometric adaptation fatigue term of Claim~D
  1617→(Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy}), which is not part of the leading-order
  1618→holonomy. Third, the qualification ``whether that holonomy is fatal''
  1619→explicitly separates the prediction of hysteresis from the prediction
  1620→of failure; the two are linked by viability margins, disturbances, and
  1621→regeneration, all of which are separate quantities.
  1622→\end{remark}

  1672→\begin{remark}[Distinction from the source's curvature-survival equivalence]
  1673→\label{rem:curv-equiv}
  1674→The proposition of this article is sharper than, and not equivalent
  1675→to, the source transcript's ``curvature-survival equivalence''.
  1676→The latter is acknowledged by the source as almost tautological
  1677→and is false in general: a flat connection can transport the system
  1678→out of the viable set, and a curved connection can keep the system
  1679→inside a large viable region. The present proposition restricts the
  1680→equivalence to leading-order hysteresis on smooth strata, separates
  1681→the hysteresis prediction from the fatality prediction, and makes
  1682→the prediction operational via the seven-claim hierarchy. The
  1683→upper-bound form ``vulnerability $\leq \kk$'' (Proposition~\ref{prop:main})
  1684→replaces the bidirectional equivalence; the lower bound $0$ is
  1685→trivially attained when $\kk$ is identically zero.
  1686→\end{remark}

  1692→\subsection{Summary of results}
  1693→
  1694→The single composition theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition})
  1695→consolidates seven arcs of prior domain-unification work into one
  1696→endofunctor on $\Optic(\CC)$. The unification object becomes a fixed
  1697→point of a well-defined endofunctor rather than a rhetorical construct;
  1698→its existence reduces to a contraction-rate question
  1699→(Section~\ref{sec:titer}), which is confirmed numerically across a
  1700→$375$-configuration robustness grid. The seven-claim falsification
  1701→hierarchy (Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy}) operationalizes the
  1702→viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ as a falsifiable prediction at seven
  1703→distinct levels, all confirmed within their stated tolerances in both
  1704→the abelian ($n=3$) and non-abelian ($n=4$) regimes.
  1705→
  1706→The heavy-tail index stress test of Section~\ref{sec:stress} establishes
  1707→that the Claim~D sufficient condition is not a single-distribution
  1708→artefact: the prediction reproduces at $f_{\mathrm{conf}}=1.000$ across
  1709→$200$ Monte-Carlo seeds at the operating scale, with a graceful
  1710→breakdown at $\sigma_\eta\geq 5\sigma_{\mathrm{op}}$. The boundary maps
  1711→cleanly onto the heavy-tail theory: the infinite-variance regime
  1712→($\mathrm{df}<2$) is broken only when the noise scale exceeds the
  1713→deterministic mean by a factor of five.
  1714→
  1715→The $n=4$ non-abelian generalization (Section~\ref{sec:n4}) confirms
  1716→that the viability-weighted curvature prediction is dimension-
  1717→independent: the leading-order scaling $\kk(a) = a^2$, the holonomy-area
  1718→scaling $c_1\sim\pi$, and the $\frac{3}{2}$-fatigue correction $c_2\sim
  1719→C_{\mathrm{fat}}$ persist without modification. The non-abelian
  1720→structure contributes only higher-order corrections, captured by two
  1721→independent falsifiable signatures: the commutator fit
  1722→$c_{\mathrm{comm}}\sim\sqrt{2}\,\pi^2$ in Claim~C
  1723→(Lemma~\ref{lem:comm}, Proposition~\ref{prop:nonab}) and the non-
  1724→commuting-sequence $T$-statistic in Claim~E. The non-abelian signature
  1725→is the $\so(3)$ commutation relation $[\mathfrak{l}_z,\mathfrak{l}_x]
  1726→= \mathfrak{l}_y$.
  1727→
  1728→\subsection{Implications and open problems}
  1729→
  1730→\paragraph{Implication 1 (RESOLVED): numerical contraction.}
  1731→The numerical contraction argument (Section~\ref{sec:titer}) is carried
  1732→out in dimension $d\leq 20$ across $375$ configurations plus an
  1733→$8$-configuration single-expansion control. The unification object
  1734→exists by Banach's fixed-point theorem throughout the tested setting.
  1735→The four WEAK configurations identified in Proposition~\ref{prop:titer}
  1736→remain contractive ($q<1$) with $R^2 < 0.9$, but a sharper
  1737→characterization of the tail-fit degradation at high Bregman strengths
  1738→and high dimension is open.
  1739→
  1740→\paragraph{Implication 2 (RESOLVED): inverse-limit RAF construction.}
  1741→The inverse-limit construction (Section~\ref{sec:invlim}) is verified
  1742→on a small reaction network ($7$ molecules, $5$ reactions). The inverse
  1743→limit equals the maximal RAF $R_{\max}$, and the viability-weighted
  1744→curvature computed via the colimit agrees with the operational
  1745→Definition~\ref{def:kappa} within machine precision ($10^{-9}$).
  1746→Extension to larger networks is in principle straightforward (filtered
  1747→colimits in $\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$ always exist) but unverified at
  1748→scale.
  1749→
  1750→\paragraph{Implication 3 (PARTIALLY RESOLVED): CPTP-Zeno lift.}
  1751→The CPTP-Zeno lift (Section~\ref{sec:cptp}) commits to a quantum-
  1752→instantiated agent; the classical Markov setting remains unresolved.
  1753→The Zeno scaling confirmation of Proposition~\ref{prop:zeno}
  1754→establishes that the CPTP lift carries an empirically distinct
  1755→signature. The binding prerequisite for full operationalization is the
  1756→construction of a quantum agent whose measurement schedules are
  1757→controllable and whose Liouvillian spectral gap is measurable; this is
  1758→an experimental commitment, supported by the empirical confirmation that
  1759→the scaling regimes are distinguishable.
  1760→
  1761→\paragraph{Implication 4 (RESOLVED): $n\geq 4$ extension.}
  1762→The $n=3$ prototype is sufficient for Claims A--E
  1763→(Section~\ref{sec:n3}) but insufficient for Claim F
  1764→(Section~\ref{sec:foundational}). The Lie algebra $\so(2)$ is
  1765→$1$-dimensional abelian, so all perturbations commute trivially at
  1766→$n=3$; the commuting-control test cannot distinguish parallel from
  1767→non-parallel rotations. Section~\ref{sec:foundational} reports the
  1768→empirical confirmation of Claim F at $n=4$; Section~\ref{sec:n4}
  1769→reports that the five derivative claims A--E generalize to the $n=4$
  1770→non-abelian regime ($\so(3)$ non-abelian) without modification of their
  1771→leading-order predictions, with the commutator signature captured as a
  1772→higher-order correction.
  1773→
  1774→\paragraph{Implication 5 (RESOLVED): quantum instantiation.}
  1775→Quantum instantiation of the agent is binding for Claim G in the
  1776→non-ergodic regime. The CPTP lift is a non-trivial quantum lift from
  1777→the classical Markov setting; the classical setting cannot test the
  1778→Zeno scaling. Section~\ref{sec:foundational} reports the empirical
  1779→confirmation of Claim G in a two-level quantum simulation; the Zeno
  1780→scaling exponent of $2$ is distinguishable from the classical scaling
  1781→exponent of $1$ by approximately a factor of $2$.
  1782→
  1783→\subsection{Limitations}
  1784→
  1785→\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  1786→\item The numerical contraction argument
  1787→(Section~\ref{sec:titer}) is carried out in dimension $d\leq 20$; the
  1788→four WEAK configurations identified in Proposition~\ref{prop:titer}
  1789→remain contractive ($q<1$) with $R^2 < 0.9$, but a sharper
  1790→characterization of the tail-fit degradation at high Bregman strengths
  1791→and high dimension is open.
  1792→\item The CPTP-Zeno lift (Section~\ref{sec:cptp}) commits to a
  1793→quantum-instantiated agent; the classical Markov setting remains
  1794→unresolved.
  1795→\item The inverse-limit construction (Section~\ref{sec:invlim}) is
  1796→verified on a small reaction network; extension to larger networks is
  1797→in principle straightforward (filtered colimits in
  1798→$\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$ always exist) but unverified at scale.
  1799→\item The SAVGS framework (Section~\ref{sec:savgs}) resolves the
  1800→constraint-switching boundary discontinuity by a $2$-categorical span;
  1801→the formal development of the $2$-categorical structure is left for
  1802→future work.
  1803→\end{enumerate}

  1830→\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
  1831→% =============================================================
  1832→
  1833→This article has constructed a single categorical framework that
  1834→unifies viability-constrained adaptation, RAF reaction networks, the
  1835→Fisher--Rao information-geometric structure of belief updates, CPTP
  1836→quantum channels with Zeno-type measurement schedules, and non-abelian
  1837→holonomy of policy loops under the structure group $\CO(n-1)$. The
  1838→framework rests on the SAVGS object
  1839→(Section~\ref{sec:savgs}), the algorithmic rate-distortion distance
  1840→(Section~\ref{sec:ard}), the Bregman-divergence Noether correspondence
  1841→(Section~\ref{sec:noether}), and the single composition theorem
  1842→(Section~\ref{sec:composition}). The viability-weighted curvature
  1843→$\kk$ is derived as the positive part of the directional derivative of
  1844→a Bregman divergence evaluated at $\distD$, and reduces to $a^2$ for
  1845→circular loops in radially symmetric viability landscapes.
  1846→
  1847→The seven-claim falsification hierarchy
  1848→(Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy}) operationalizes $\kk$ as a falsifiable
  1849→prediction at seven distinct levels. All seven claims are confirmed
  1850→within their stated tolerances: the foundational claims F and G in
  1851→Section~\ref{sec:foundational}, the derivative claims A--E in the
  1852→$n=3$ prototype in Section~\ref{sec:n3}, the heavy-tail stress test of
  1853→Claim~D in Section~\ref{sec:stress}, and the non-abelian
  1854→generalization of A--E to $n=4$ in Section~\ref{sec:n4}. The
  1855→unification object exists by Banach's fixed-point theorem across the
  1856→$375$-configuration robustness grid of Section~\ref{sec:titer}; the
  1857→inverse-limit RAF construction of Section~\ref{sec:invlim} produces the
  1858→maximal RAF as the colimit, with viability-weighted curvature matching
  1859→the operational form to machine precision.
  1860→
  1861→The main proposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:main}) states the joint
  1862→thesis in its strongest defensible form: adaptive systems are
  1863→endangered not by large environmental changes but by non-commuting
  1864→sequences of individually manageable changes whose induced policy
  1865→holonomy aligns with vulnerable self-maintenance directions; the upper
  1866→bound on vulnerability is the algorithmic-rate-distortion-theoretic
  1867→viability-weighted curvature on a $\CO(n-1)$-structured stratified
  1868→connection. The proposition is sharper than the thesis in three
  1869→respects (Remark~\ref{rem:sharpenings}): the smooth constant-active-set
  1870→strata are the domain of definition; the leading-order prediction is
  1871→explicitly leading-order; the prediction of hysteresis is separated
  1872→from the prediction of failure. The proposition is falsifiable via the
  1873→seven-claim hierarchy, and all seven claims are confirmed.
