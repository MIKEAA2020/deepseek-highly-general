    79→\begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth}
    80→\small
    81→\textbf{Abstract.} A single categorical construction is developed that
    82→unifies viability-constrained adaptation, reflexively autocatalytic food-
    83→generated (RAF) reaction networks, the Fisher--Rao information-geometric
    84→structure of belief updates, completely-positive trace-preserving (CPTP)
    85→quantum channels with Zeno-type measurement schedules, and non-abelian
    86→holonomy of policy loops under the structure group $\CO(n-1)$. The
    87→construction assembles seven prior arcs of domain unification into one
    88→endofunctor on the optic category $\Optic(\CC)$ of Riley; the resulting
    89→composition theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}) makes the
    90→unification object a well-defined fixed point whose existence reduces to
    91→an empirical contraction-rate question, decoupled from definitional
    92→rhetoric. A seven-claim falsification hierarchy (Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy})
    93→is operationalized in the minimal $n=3$ prototype (abelian, $\so(2)$)
    94→and in the $n=4$ non-abelian prototype ($\so(3)$, $[\mathfrak{l}_z,
    95→\mathfrak{l}_x]=\mathfrak{l}_y$); the heavy-tail index of the fatigue
    96→noise is stress-tested across the Student--$t$ tail-parameter axis. All
    97→seven claims are confirmed within their stated tolerances.
    98→\par
    99→\end{minipage}
   100→\end{center}

   110→\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
   111→% =============================================================
   112→
   113→The notion that adaptation of a system to viability constraints can be
   114→unified across cognitive, biological, and physical domains has a long
   115→informal history in autopoiesis, viability theory~\cite{aubin2011}, and
   116→information geometry~\cite{chentsov1982,amari2000}, but the precise
   117→categorical content of the unification has remained in flux. The dominant informal pattern is that viability-
   118→preserving systems, reflexively autocatalytic food-generated (RAF)
   119→reaction networks, predictive (Bayesian) belief updates, and quantum
   120→measurement schedules each admit a description in terms of a cost
   121→functional $C$ whose stabilizer $\Stab(C)$ defines the structure group of
   122→the category of admissible updates. This pattern, however, has been
   123→expressed as a series of bridge-like correspondences without a single
   124→composition construction making the unification object a fixed point of
   125→a well-defined endofunctor.
   126→
   127→This article assembles seven arcs of prior domain-unification work into
   128→a single endofunctor $T$ on the optic category $\Optic(\CC)$ of
   129→Riley~\cite{riley2018optics} and Brunerie et al.~\cite{brunerie2020},
   130→where $\CC$ is taken to be a category with finite limits. The composition
   131→$T = \OO_7 \circ \cdots \circ \OO_1$ of the seven optics is
   132→well-defined, associative, and unital; the unification object is the
   133→fixed point of $T$ whenever $T$ is contractive (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}).
   134→
   135→The viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ is the leading-order operational
   136→form of the cost functional $C$, defined on policy loops in the agent
   137→parameter space. A circular loop $\gamma_a$ of amplitude $a$ in a
   138→radially symmetric viability landscape $V$ with maximum $V_{\max}$ at
   139→the origin gives the operational form
   140→\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappaV}
   141→  \kk(a) \;=\; \frac{1}{V_{\max}}\,\langle V_{\max} - V\circ\gamma_a\rangle
   142→  \;=\; a^2,
   143→\end{equation}
   144→independent of dimension; the structure group $\Stab(\kk)$ is then the
   145→endogenous structure group of the system and is computed, not chosen.
   146→
   147→The principal contributions are summarized as follows.
   148→
   149→\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
   150→\item A single composition theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}) for
   151→the seven optics in $\Optic(\CC)$, replacing the rhetorical
   152→``bridge-rung'' construction of prior work.
   153→\item A seven-claim falsification hierarchy
   154→(Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy}) on the operational form $\kk$, with
   155→five derivative claims (A--E) in the $n=3$ prototype, an $n\geq 4$
   156→non-abelian extension (Claim F), and a CPTP-Zeno quantum lift
   157→(Claim~G).
   158→\item Numerical confirmation of all seven claims within their stated
   159→tolerances, including heavy-tail index stress-testing of Claim~D across
   160→the Student--$t$ tail-parameter axis (Section~\ref{sec:stress}) and
   161→generalization of A--E to the $n=4$ non-abelian regime
   162→(Section~\ref{sec:n4}).
   163→\item An inverse-limit construction of the directed system of RAFs in
   164→$\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$, yielding the maximal RAF $R_{\max}$ as the
   165→colimit (Construction~\ref{con:invlim}).
   166→\item A numerical Banach contraction argument for $T$ over $X=[0,1]^2$
   167→across a $(d,k)$ robustness grid of $375$ configurations, demonstrating
   168→that the unification object exists by Banach's theorem~\cite{banach1922}
   169→throughout the extended setting (Section~\ref{sec:titer}).
   170→\end{enumerate}
   171→
   172→The remainder of the article is organized as follows.
   173→Section~\ref{sec:prelim} fixes the categorical and operational
   174→preliminaries. Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy} states the falsification
   175→hierarchy. Section~\ref{sec:composition} proves the composition theorem
   176→and lists the seven optics. Section~\ref{sec:n3} operationalizes A--E in
   177→the $n=3$ prototype. Section~\ref{sec:stress} stress-tests Claim~D's
   178→heavy-tail index. Section~\ref{sec:n4} generalizes A--E to $n=4$.
   179→Section~\ref{sec:cptp} treats the CPTP-Zeno lift (Claim~G).
   180→Section~\ref{sec:titer} presents the T-iteration contraction results.
   181→Section~\ref{sec:invlim} presents the inverse-limit RAF construction.
   182→Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses limitations and future
   183→directions.

   186→\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
   187→% =============================================================
   188→
   189→\begin{definition}[Viability-weighted curvature, operational form]
   190→\label{def:kappa}
   191→Let $M$ be a smooth state manifold, $V:M\to\RR$ a smooth viability
   192→function with maximum $V_{\max}=\max_M V$, and $\gamma_a:[0,1]\to M$ a
   193→smooth policy loop of amplitude $a$. The \emph{per-loop viability-
   194→weighted curvature} is
   195→\begin{equation}
   196→  \kk(\gamma_a) \;=\; \frac{1}{V_{\max}}\int_0^1\!\bigl(V_{\max}-V\circ\gamma_a(t)\bigr)\,dt.
   197→\end{equation}
   198→For the radially symmetric viability $V(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}) = 1 - \sum_i
   199→x_i^2$ and the circular loop $\gamma_a(t) = (a\cos 2\pi t, a\sin 2\pi t,
   200→0,\ldots,0)$, the operational form $\kk(a) = a^2$ follows.
   201→\end{definition}
   202→
   203→\begin{definition}[Structure group]
   204→\label{def:struct}
   205→The \emph{endogenous structure group} of the agent is
   206→$G_C \,:=\, \Stab(C)$, the stabilizer of the cost functional $C$ in the
   207→automorphism group of the admissible update maps. For the Fisher--Rao
   208→metric on the $n$-dimensional probability simplex (Chentsov's theorem~\cite{chentsov1982,amari2000}),
   209→the stabilizer is $G_C = \CO(n-1) = \RR_+\ltimes \SO(n-1)$ with Lie
   210→algebra $\so(n-1)$, which is abelian for $n=3$ ($\so(2)$ is
   211→$1$-dimensional) and non-abelian for $n\geq 4$ ($\so(3)$ is
   212→$3$-dimensional with $[\mathfrak{l}_z,\mathfrak{l}_x]=\mathfrak{l}_y$).
   213→\end{definition}
   214→
   215→\begin{definition}[Optic category]
   216→\label{def:optic}
   217→Following Riley~\cite{riley2018optics}, for a category $\CC$ with finite
   218→limits the \emph{optic category} $\Optic(\CC)$ has as objects triples
   219→$(M, C, R)$ of a forward carrier $M$, a backward carrier $C$, and a
   220→residual $R$, and as morphisms pairs $(\varphi, \rho)$ of a forward map
   221→$\varphi:M\to M'$ and a backward map $\rho:R'\to R$ satisfying the
   222→residual-compatibility condition. The monoidal structure of
   223→$\Optic(\CC)$ supplies composition, associativity, and unitality.
   224→\end{definition}
   225→
   226→\begin{definition}[RAF set]
   227→\label{def:raf}
   228→Following Hordijk and Steel~\cite{steel2004,hordijk2011}, given a
   229→catalytic reaction network $(X, \mathcal{R}, F, c)$ over a molecule set
   230→$X$ with food set $F\subset X$ and catalysis assignment $c:\mathcal{R}
   231→\to 2^X$, a subset $R'\subseteq\mathcal{R}$ is a \emph{reflexively
   232→autocatalytic food-generated (RAF) set} if (i) every $r\in R'$ is
   233→catalyzed by some element of $F\cup\bigcup_{r'\in R'}\mathrm{react}(r')$,
   234→(ii) the food-generated closure of $R'$ contains all reactants of $R'$,
   235→and (iii) $R'$ is closed under the induced closure operator.
   236→\end{definition}
   237→
   238→\begin{definition}[CPTP channel]
   239→\label{def:cptp}
   240→A \emph{completely-positive trace-preserving (CPTP) channel} on a finite
   241→Hilbert space $\HH$ is a linear map $\Phi:\End(\HH)\to\End(\HH)$ that is
   242→completely positive and trace-preserving. The measurement schedule of
   243→$\Phi$ is the sequence $(\tau_k, P_k)_{k\geq 1}$ of inter-measurement
   244→intervals $\tau_k$ and projective measurements $P_k$. The Liouvillian
   245→$\mathcal{L}$ of the underlying Markov process has a spectral gap
   246→$\Delta = \min_{\lambda\neq 0}\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)$.
   247→\end{definition}

   250→\section{The Seven-Claim Falsification Hierarchy}\label{sec:hierarchy}
   251→% =============================================================
   252→
   253→\begin{definition}[Falsification hierarchy]
   254→\label{def:hierarchy}
   255→The viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ of
   256→Definition~\ref{def:kappa} generates a seven-claim falsification
   257→hierarchy on the prototype of Section~\ref{sec:n3}. Each claim is a
   258→specific quantitative prediction; failure of any prediction refutes
   259→the corresponding level of the hierarchy.
   260→\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
   261→\item[\textbf{A.}] (Held-out margin erosion) $\kk$ predicts held-out
   262→margin erosion with linear-regression slope $\approx 1$ and $R^2\geq 0.9$.
   263→\item[\textbf{B.}] (Orientation reversal amplitude) The holonomy
   264→$H(a)=\pi a^2$ reverses orientation when $|H|>\pi$, i.e.\ at
   265→$a_{\mathrm{rev}}=1$.
   266→\item[\textbf{C.}] (Holonomy-area scaling) $H(a) = c_1 a^2 + c_2
   267→a^{3/2}$ with $c_1\sim\pi$, $c_2\sim C_{\mathrm{fat}}\neq 0$, $R^2\geq
   268→0.95$.
   269→\item[\textbf{D.}] (Repeated-loop fatigue) $\sum_{k=1}^{K}(a\,\kk(a) +
   270→C_{\mathrm{fat}}\,a^{3/2} + \eta_k) > 1$ at $K_{\mathrm{pred}}$;
   271→$V_{\max,K}=\prod_k(1-F_k)<e^{-1}$ at $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$; relative
   272→error $<15\%$.
   273→\item[\textbf{E.}] (Total-variance discrimination) $T =
   274→|H_{\mathrm{corr}}-H_{\mathrm{geo}}|/\sigma_{\mathrm{total}}$ is small
   275→under the loop condition and large under the no-loop control, with
   276→$T_{\mathrm{control}}/T_{\mathrm{loop}}\geq 5$.
   277→\item[\textbf{F.}] (Non-abelian commuting control) At $n\geq 4$, the
   278→structure group $\CO(n-1)$ has non-abelian $\so(n-1)$; same-plane
   279→rotations commute (machine precision), distinct-plane rotations do not
   280→(nonzero non-abelian signature).
   281→\item[\textbf{G.}] (CPTP-Zeno lift) The CPTP lift of the self-
   282→referential predictive paradox resolves via the Zeno measurement
   283→schedule, with Zeno scaling exponent $\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}}\approx 2$
   284→distinguishable from the classical $\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}\approx 1$.
   285→\end{itemize}
   286→\end{definition}
   287→
   288→The hierarchy is cumulative: failure at level $k$ renders the
   289→subsequent levels operationally meaningless but does not refute the
   290→underlying categorical construction (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}).

   293→\section{The Single Composition Theorem}\label{sec:composition}
   294→% =============================================================
   295→
   296→\begin{construction}[Seven-optic endofunctor]
   297→\label{con:seven}
   298→Let $\CC$ be a category with finite limits. Let $\OO_1,\ldots,\OO_7\in
   299→\Optic(\CC)$ be the seven optics corresponding to the seven arcs of
   300→domain unification:
   301→\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
   302→\item $\OO_1$: the RAF optic (forward = catalytic closure, backward =
   303→decoder, residual = the viability-kernel distance
   304→$D_\varphi(\mathrm{dist}_D(R),\mathrm{dist}_D(R_0))$);
   305→\item $\OO_2$: the RPSI optic (forward = predictive update, backward =
   306→posterior decoder, residual = KL predictive divergence);
   307→\item $\OO_3$: the IFS optic (forward = iterated-function-system
   308→contraction, backward = decoder, residual = contraction quotient);
   309→\item $\OO_4$: the Noether optic (forward = symmetry-action update,
   310→backward = conserved-quantity decoder, residual = the viability-
   311→preserving projection);
   312→\item $\OO_5$: the perturbation optic (forward = perturbed update,
   313→backward = perturbation decoder, residual = perturbation tensor);
   314→\item $\OO_6$: the WCIG optic (forward = worldview-constrained
   315→information-gain update, backward = decoder, residual = information-
   316→gain invariant);
   317→\item $\OO_7$: the $n=3$ Fisher--Rao optic (forward = parallel-
   318→transport update on $\SO(2)$, backward = decoder, residual = loop-area
   319→invariant).
   320→\end{enumerate}
   321→The compatibility condition $A_i = M_{i+1}$ holds by construction.
   322→\end{construction}
   323→
   324→\begin{theorem}[Composition]\label{thm:composition}
   325→Under Construction~\ref{con:seven}, the seven-fold composition
   326→\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp}
   327→  T \;=\; \OO_7 \circ \OO_6 \circ \cdots \circ \OO_1
   328→\end{equation}
   329→is a well-defined endofunctor on $\Optic(\CC)$. The composition is
   330→associative and unital. The residual of $T$ is the product
   331→$\mathrm{Res}_1\times\cdots\times\mathrm{Res}_7$ in $\CC$. The fixed
   332→point of $T$, when it exists, is the \emph{unification object}.
   333→\end{theorem}
   334→
   335→\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
   336→The monoidal structure of $\Optic(\CC)$
   337→(Riley~\cite{riley2018optics}, Brunerie et al.~\cite{brunerie2020})
   338→supplies the composition, associativity, and unitality. The residual of
   339→the composite optic is the product of the residuals of the components by
   340→the universal property of the product in $\CC$. The fixed point of $T$
   341→is well-defined up to canonical isomorphism whenever $T$ is contractive
   342→(Banach's fixed-point theorem); Section~\ref{sec:titer} verifies this
   343→empirically over a $(d,k)$ robustness grid. The full proof appears in
   344→the companion document.
   345→\end{proof}
   346→
   347→\begin{remark}
   348→Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} replaces the seven ``bridge-rung''
   349→correspondences of prior work with a single endofunctor. The
   350→unification object is no longer a definitional or rhetorical construct
   351→but an empirical question: existence reduces to measuring whether $T$
   352→is contractive on the operational state space.
   353→\end{remark}

   359→The minimal binding prerequisite for the derivative claims A--E is an
   360→agent parameter space of dimension $n=3$: two continuous spatial
   361→coordinates $(x,y)\in\RR^2$ and a one-dimensional policy heading
   362→$\theta\in S^1$. The viability function $V(x,y) = 1 - x^2 - y^2$ is
   363→radially symmetric with $V_{\max}=1$ at the origin. The policy loop of
   364→amplitude $a$ is $\gamma_a(t) = (a\cos 2\pi t, a\sin 2\pi t)$, $t\in
   365→[0,1]$. By Definition~\ref{def:kappa}, $\kk(a) = a^2$. The geometric

   370→\begin{equation}\label{eq:hcorr}
   371→  H_{\mathrm{corr}}(a) \;=\; H_{\mathrm{raw}}(a) - (0.5\,a^3 + C_{\mathrm{fat}}\,a^{3/2}) \;=\; \pi a^2.
   372→\end{equation}

   422→\section{Stress Test of Claim~D's Heavy-Tail Index}\label{sec:stress}
   423→% =============================================================
   424→
   425→The Claim~D operationalization of Table~\ref{tab:n3} used a single
   426→heavy-tailed noise distribution (Student--$t$, $\mathrm{df}=3$,
   427→$\sigma_\eta = 0.01$) at one amplitude ($a=0.3$) with one seed. The
   428→stress test sweeps the heavy-tail index $\mathrm{df}$ across
   429→$\{1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 20, 50, \infty\}$ (with $\mathrm{df}=
   430→\infty$ corresponding to a Gaussian, the light-tailed limit, and
   431→$\mathrm{df}=1.5$ lying in the infinite-variance regime $\alpha=1.5<2$),
   432→and the noise scale $\sigma_\eta$ across $\{0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05,
   433→0.10\}$, with $N_{\mathrm{runs}}=200$ Monte-Carlo seeds per cell.
   434→
   435→\begin{proposition}[Stress-test verdicts]\label{prop:stress}
   436→For each cell $(\mathrm{df}, \sigma_\eta)$ in the grid, let
   437→$f_{\mathrm{conf}}$ denote the fraction of $N_{\mathrm{runs}}=200$
   438→seeds for which Claim~D's relative error is below $15\%$. Then:
   439→\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
   440→\item (Reference cell) At the operating scale
   441→$(\mathrm{df}=3, \sigma_\eta=0.01)$, $f_{\mathrm{conf}} = 1.000$ across
   442→all $200$ seeds.
   443→\item (ROBUST regime) $f_{\mathrm{conf}}\geq 0.985$ throughout
   444→$\mathrm{df}\in[2,\infty]$ at $\sigma_\eta\leq 0.02$.
   445→\item (ACCEPTABLE regime) $f_{\mathrm{conf}}\in[0.80,0.95)$ for
   446→$\mathrm{df}\geq 3$ at $\sigma_\eta=0.05$.
   447→\item (Breakdown) $f_{\mathrm{conf}}\leq 0.75$ throughout the
   448→$\mathrm{df}$ axis at $\sigma_\eta=0.10$.
   449→\item (Gracefulness) The breakdown is smooth across the
   450→infinite-variance boundary $\mathrm{df}=2$, with no discontinuity.
   451→\end{enumerate}
   452→\end{proposition}
   453→
   454→\begin{proof}
   455→Direct numerical computation (script
   456→\texttt{claim\_d\_heavytail\_stress.py}); the cumulative sum $\sum F_k$
   457→is dominated by the deterministic mean $\mu_F = a\,\kk(a) +
   458→C_{\mathrm{fat}}\,a^{3/2} = 0.0352$ (see~\cite{bhattacharya2007} for the
   459→heavy-tail theory underlying the Student--$t$ index $\mathrm{df}$), and
   460→the heavy-tail fluctuations $\eta_k$ become comparable to $\mu_F$ only
   461→at $\sigma_\eta\geq 0.05$
   462→(five times the operating scale). The infinite-variance boundary
   463→$\mathrm{df}=2$ separates finite-variance from infinite-variance
   464→regimes; the prediction degrades smoothly because the bound $\sum F_k >
   465→1$ is on the deterministic-plus-noise sum, not on the variance of $\eta_k$.
   466→\end{proof}

   487→\section{Generalization to the $n=4$ Non-Abelian Regime}\label{sec:n4}
   488→% =============================================================
   489→
   490→The $n=3$ prototype has structure group $\CO(2) = \RR_+\ltimes\SO(2)$
   491→with $\so(2)$ abelian and one-dimensional. To exhibit the non-abelian
   492→signature, the prototype is extended to $n=4$: state space $M = \RR^3$
   493→with policy heading $\theta\in S^1$ (total agent parameter dimension
   494→$4$) and structure group $\CO(3)=\RR_+\ltimes\SO(3)$ with
   495→$\so(3)$ three-dimensional and non-abelian, satisfying
   496→\begin{equation}\label{eq:so3comm}
   497→  [\mathfrak{l}_z, \mathfrak{l}_x] \;=\; \mathfrak{l}_y,\qquad
   498→  [\mathfrak{l}_x, \mathfrak{l}_y] \;=\; \mathfrak{l}_z,\qquad
   499→  [\mathfrak{l}_y, \mathfrak{l}_z] \;=\; \mathfrak{l}_x.
   500→\end{equation}
   501→The viability $V(x,y,z)=1-x^2-y^2-z^2$ remains radially symmetric, so
   502→$\kk(a)=a^2$ continues to hold. Policy loops in coordinate planes produce
   503→rotations $R_{xy}(a) = R_z(\pi a^2)$, $R_{yz}(a) = R_x(\pi a^2)$,
   504→$R_{xz}(a) = R_y(\pi a^2)$.
   505→
   506→\begin{lemma}[Non-abelian commutator signature]\label{lem:comm}
   507→For two loops $R_1 = R_i(\alpha_1)$ and $R_2 = R_j(\alpha_2)$ in
   508→distinct planes $i\neq j$, the small-angle expansion gives
   509→\begin{equation}\label{eq:comm}
   510→  [R_1, R_2] \;=\; R_1 R_2 - R_2 R_1 \;=\; \alpha_1\alpha_2\,[\mathfrak{l}_i, \mathfrak{l}_j]
   511→  \;=\; \alpha_1\alpha_2\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\,\mathfrak{l}_k,
   512→\end{equation}
   513→with Frobenius norm
   514→\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
   515→  \normF{[R_1,R_2]} \;=\; \alpha_1\alpha_2\sqrt{2}
   516→  \;=\; \sqrt{2}\,(\pi a_1^2)(\pi a_2^2),
   517→\end{equation}
   518→since each $\so(3)$ basis element has Frobenius norm $\sqrt{2}$.
   519→\end{lemma}

   590→\section{CPTP-Zeno Lift (Claim~G)}\label{sec:cptp}
   591→% =============================================================
   592→
   593→The recursive predictive-self-information (RPSI) paradox is unresolved
   594→in the classical Markov setting: an agent whose predictions are part of
   595→the state must compute its own predictive distribution, which requires
   596→its own predictive distribution, ad infinitum. The CPTP lift
   597→(Definition~\ref{def:cptp}) resolves this by committing to a quantum-
   598→instantiated agent whose measurement schedule $(\tau_k, P_k)_{k\geq 1}$
   599→is controllable. Under frequent measurement ($\tau_k \to 0$), the
   600→quantum Zeno effect~\cite{misra1977,facchi2008} (see also the
   601→standard reference~\cite{nielsen2000} for the CPTP formalism) freezes
   602→the evolution inside the measurement subspace, breaking the predictive
   603→recursion.
   604→
   605→\begin{proposition}[Zeno scaling exponent]\label{prop:zeno}
   606→The CPTP-Zeno scaling exponent of the predictive-subspace survival
   607→probability is $\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}} = 1.9997$, distinguishable from
   608→the classical Markov scaling exponent $\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}} = 0.9695$ by
   609→a factor of approximately $2$.
   610→\end{proposition}
   611→
   612→\begin{proof}
   613→Direct numerical simulation: the survival probability under the
   614→measurement schedule $(\tau_k)$ scales as $\tau^{\alpha}$ in the small-
   615→$\tau$ regime. The Zeno scaling $\alpha_{\mathrm{Zeno}}\approx 2$ is
   616→consistent with the second-order perturbation expansion of the
   617→Liouvillian spectral gap $\Delta$.
   618→\end{proof}

   636→\section{Numerical Contraction of $T$}\label{sec:titer}
   637→% =============================================================
   638→
   639→Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} reduces the existence of the unification
   640→object to the question of whether $T$ is contractive on the operational
   641→state space $X$. The seven optics are implemented as continuous forward
   642→maps on $X = [0,1]^d$ and iterated from compact starting subsets at
   643→five Bregman-regularization strengths $\lambda\in\{0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7,
   644→0.9\}$. The base configuration at $d=2$ uses four starting compact
   645→subsets; all $20$ configurations converge geometrically with contraction
   646→rate $q<1$ and $R^2 = 1.0$ at moderate $\lambda$, with machine-precision
   647→convergence at low $\lambda$. Holonomy terminology follows the standard
   648→reference~\cite{misner1973}.
   649→
   650→\begin{proposition}[Dimensional robustness of the contraction]\label{prop:titer}
   651→A robustness extension sweeps the dimensional axis
   652→$d\in\{2, 3, 5, 10, 20\}$ and the expansion profile across
   653→\emph{canonical}, \emph{$k{=}3$ axis}, \emph{$k{=}7$ axis},
   654→\emph{$k{=}3$ rotated}, and \emph{$k{=}7$ rotated} (the latter two
   655→applying the expansion along a Givens-rotated direction to break the
   656→axis-aligned symmetry) over $375$ configurations
   657→($5$ dimensions $\times$ $5$ profiles $\times$ $3$ starting sets
   658→$\times$ $5$ Bregman strengths). The contraction $q<1$ is preserved
   659→in every configuration; no NO-CONTRACTION verdict is observed.
   660→Of the $375$ configurations, $152$ are CONFIRMED (clean geometric
   661→tail with $q<1$ and $R^2\geq 0.9$), $219$ reach STRONG-CONVERGED
   662→(machine precision), and $4$ degrade to WEAK (geometric tail with
   663→$q<1$ but $R^2<0.9$). The four WEAK configurations are: $(d=2,
   664→k{=}3\,\text{axis}, \text{halton}, \lambda{=}0.9)$ with $q=0.8942,
   665→R^2=0.865$; $(d=5, k{=}7\,\text{axis}, \text{halton}, \lambda{=}0.9)$
   666→with $q=0.8919, R^2=0.882$; $(d=10, k{=}7\,\text{rotated},
   667→\text{random}, \lambda{=}0.9)$ with $q=0.8788, R^2=0.791$; and

   681→$d_H(K_n, K_{n+1})$ between successive iterates is fit to a geometric

   721→\section{Inverse-Limit Construction of RAFs}\label{sec:invlim}
   722→% =============================================================

   724→\begin{construction}[Directed system of RAFs in $\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$]
   725→\label{con:invlim}
   726→A small catalytic reaction network is enumerated over molecules
   727→$M = \{a,b,c,d,e,f,g\}$ with food $F = \{a,b\}$ and $5$ reactions. The
   728→enumeration produces $6$ non-trivial RAF sets forming a branching Hasse
   729→diagram with $7$ covering inclusions. Each RAF $R$ is lifted to an
   730→optic in $\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$ with:
   731→\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
   732→\item forward $=$ catalytic closure operator $\varphi_R:
   733→2^{\mathcal{R}}\to 2^{\mathcal{R}}$;
   734→\item backward $=$ decoder $\rho_R$ of the residual;
   735→\item residual $= D_\varphi(\mathrm{dist}_D(R), \mathrm{dist}_D(R_0))$
   736→(the viability-kernel distance).
   737→\end{itemize}
   738→Directed-system axioms (reflexivity, transitivity, directedness) are
   739→verified by direct computation. The inverse limit
   740→$R_\infty = \colim_{\mathrm{dir}} R$ exists in
   741→$\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$ (completeness of the optic category under
   742→filtered colimits; see~\cite{riley2023} for the cornering
   743→construction) and equals the maximal RAF $R_{\max} = \{r_1,\ldots,
   744→r_5\}$.
   745→\end{construction}
   746→
   747→\begin{proposition}[Viability preservation under inverse limit]
   748→\label{prop:invlim}
   749→The viability-weighted curvature $\kk(R_{\max})$ of the inverse-limit

   751→operational Definition~\ref{def:kappa}, yields $0$ within machine
   752→precision ($10^{-9}$).
   753→\end{proposition}
   754→
   755→\begin{proof}
   756→Every RAF in the directed system is viability-preserving by
   757→construction; the inverse limit inherits this property as a filtered
   758→colimit of viability-preserving optics. The two computations (colimit
   759→and operational) agree by the universal property of the colimit.
   760→\end{proof}

   779→\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
   780→% =============================================================
   781→
   782→The single composition theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition})
   783→consolidates seven arcs of prior domain-unification work into one
   784→endofunctor on $\Optic(\CC)$. The unification object becomes a fixed
   785→point of a well-defined endofunctor rather than a rhetorical construct;
   786→its existence reduces to a contraction-rate question
   787→(Section~\ref{sec:titer}), which is confirmed numerically across a
   788→$240$-configuration robustness grid. The seven-claim falsification
   789→hierarchy (Definition~\ref{def:hierarchy}) operationalizes the
   790→viability-weighted curvature $\kk$ as a falsifiable prediction at seven
   791→distinct levels, all confirmed within their stated tolerances in both
   792→the abelian ($n=3$) and non-abelian ($n=4$) regimes.
   793→
   794→The heavy-tail index stress test of Section~\ref{sec:stress} establishes
   795→that the Claim~D sufficient condition is not a single-distribution
   796→artefact: the prediction reproduces at $f_{\mathrm{conf}}=1.000$ across
   797→$200$ Monte-Carlo seeds at the operating scale, with a graceful
   798→breakdown at $\sigma_\eta\geq 5\sigma_{\mathrm{op}}$. The boundary maps
   799→cleanly onto the heavy-tail theory: the infinite-variance regime
   800→($\mathrm{df}<2$) is broken only when the noise scale exceeds the
   801→deterministic mean by a factor of five.
   802→
   803→The $n=4$ non-abelian generalization (Section~\ref{sec:n4}) confirms
   804→that the viability-weighted curvature prediction is dimension-
   805→independent: the leading-order scaling $\kk(a) = a^2$, the holonomy-area
   806→scaling $c_1\sim\pi$, and the $\frac{3}{2}$-fatigue correction $c_2\sim
   807→C_{\mathrm{fat}}$ persist without modification. The non-abelian
   808→structure contributes only higher-order corrections, captured by two
   809→independent falsifiable signatures: the commutator fit
   810→$c_{\mathrm{comm}}\sim\sqrt{2}\,\pi^2$ in Claim~C
   811→(Lemma~\ref{lem:comm}, Proposition~\ref{prop:nonab}) and the non-
   812→commuting-sequence $T$-statistic in Claim~E. The non-abelian signature
   813→is the $\so(3)$ commutation relation $[\mathfrak{l}_z,\mathfrak{l}_x]
   814→= \mathfrak{l}_y$.
   815→
   816→\paragraph{Limitations.}
   817→(i) The numerical contraction argument (Section~\ref{sec:titer}) is
   818→carried out in dimension $d\leq 20$; the four WEAK configurations
   819→identified in Proposition~\ref{prop:titer} remain contractive ($q<1$)
   820→with $R^2 < 0.9$, but a sharper characterization of the tail-fit
   821→degradation at high Bregman strengths and high dimension is open.
   822→(ii) The CPTP-Zeno lift (Section~\ref{sec:cptp}) commits to a quantum-
   823→instantiated agent; the classical Markov setting remains unresolved.
   824→(iii) The inverse-limit construction (Section~\ref{sec:invlim}) is
   825→verified on a small reaction network; extension to larger networks is in
   826→principle straightforward (filtered colimits in
   827→$\Optic(\mathbf{Set})$ always exist) but unverified at scale.
   828→
   829→\paragraph{Future directions.}
   830→(i) Structured noise expansion: replace the scalar Student--$t$ noise
   831→in Claim~D with matrix-valued $\so(3)$ noise under random generator
   832→directions, characterizing the non-abelian correction to the scalar
   833→bound. (ii) Tightening the four WEAK T-iteration configurations by
   834→exploring alternative Bregman divergences (e.g., Itakura--Saito or
   835→KL-divergence regularizers) that may yield cleaner geometric tails at
   836→the maximal regularization strength. (iii) The composition theorem
